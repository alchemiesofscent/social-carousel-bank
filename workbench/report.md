## `2026-03-17-wp3`

Strong `DEAD WORDS, LIVING SCENTS` identity. The best entries turn lexical detail into something bodily and memorable. The main weakness is compression: several posts ask the reader to absorb too much Greek, too many examples, or too much aphoristic punch in too little space.

- `euodia-dysodes`: strong diagnostic/forensic frame and a serious voice. Issue: the hook overpromises with “The other was a cure”; the body proves authentication rather than cure.
- `plektikon`: probably the cleanest carousel in the set; one idea, one body slide, immediate payoff. Issue: “asked questions later” feels a little too copywriter-ish for the account voice.
- `baryosmon-bromodes`: good conceptual distinction between two kinds of bad smell. Issue: the densest read in the folder; too many terms, examples, and quotation load before the closer.
- `izo-verbs-adulteration`: distinctive and intellectually interesting, with an excellent closer. Issue: the middle is too abstract for first-pass reading; morphology, fraud logic, and examples arrive at once.

Top revision priorities:

- Fix hook/body alignment in `euodia-dysodes`.
- Reduce lexical load in `baryosmon-bromodes` and `izo-verbs-adulteration`.
- Pull back the cutest phrasing in `plektikon`.
- Vary closers so more of them reframe instead of summarizing.

## Combined Synthesis

### Cross-folder strengths

- Hooks are consistently strong across the bank. The project knows how to lead with discovery, specific numbers, strange contrasts, and concrete surprise.
- The best posts translate scholarship into physical, social, or sensory terms. Bodily language, scenes, prices, tools, and movement through space reliably improve readability.
- The strongest entries have a clean spine and a shaped payoff rather than just preserved source density. Best examples include `plektikon`, `filling-the-nose`, `kyphi-solar-lunar`, `balsam-judea`, `pliny-royal-perfume`, `blind-perfumers`, `perfumers-shade`, `castor-linseed`, and `moschos`.
- Closers are often strong at the line level. Even where they repeat a habit, they are usually concise and memorable.

### Recurring writing issues

- The main recurring problem is middle-slide density. Across folders, the weakest drafts ask the reader to process too many names, units, terms, clauses, quotations, or examples on one screen.
- Hooks are often stronger than bodies. Several posts open with a sharp promise but cash it out through accumulation instead of shaped development.
- Closers too often summarize cleanly instead of producing a fresher final turn. This appears in WP3, WP4, WP9a, and parts of WP6 overflow.
- Some batches lean too heavily on repeated structural habits: list, list, then reframe; or sharp fact, dense explanation, modern aphoristic closer.
- A few folders drift into slightly modernized or internet-coded language that feels glib next to the project’s stronger PBS Eons register.

### Accessibility and readability findings

- Proper-name load is a recurring friction point. Body slides often stack authors, places, and technical nouns before the reader has a plain-language anchor.
- Greek and Latin work best when used sparingly and strategically. They start to hurt first-pass comprehension when transliteration, translation, and argument arrive together.
- Several posts preserve more of the research note texture than an Instagram carousel can comfortably carry. This is most visible in overloaded recipe posts, marketplace supply-chain posts, and overflow-split revisions.
- The most readable carousels usually do one clear thing per slide and cash lists out into image, consequence, or social meaning immediately.

### Strongest exemplars

- Strongest hook: `blind-perfumers`, `castor-linseed`, `tus-supply-chain`, `root-cutters`
- Strongest flow: `plektikon`, `kyphi-solar-lunar`, `pliny-royal-perfume`, `moschos`
- Best audience fit: `cleopatra-regime`, `piper-gold`, `rose-garland-medicine`, `perfumers-shade`
- Best closers: `castor-linseed`, `perfume-and-soul`, `perfumers-shade`, `mendesian-paul`

### Highest-priority weak spots

- `falernian-scale`: biggest accessibility problem in the reviewed set; too many comparisons and too much nested explanation.
- `aetius-people`: strongest example of accumulation replacing shape.
- `kyphi-edfu`: strongest example of body text reading like compressed research notes.
- `baryosmon-bromodes` and `izo-verbs-adulteration`: strongest examples of lexical and conceptual overload.
- `hicesius-drinking-perfumes`: most list-like execution in WP4.
- `new-spices` and `karyophyllon`: strongest cases where overflow splitting weakened structure.

### Guidance for future drafting

- Apply a hard body-necessity check. If covering one body slide does not break the argument, cut or merge it.
- Limit unfamiliar name and term load on any one slide unless comparison is the entire point.
- Any list slide should cash out immediately into consequence, image, argument, or scene.
- Treat closers as reframes, not summaries. A clean summary is not enough if it does not open a final angle.
- Prefer one vivid example over three compressed ones when mobile readability is at risk.
- Watch tonal drift into punchline language, internet rhetoric, or contemporary-marketing phrasing.
- In overflow revisions, avoid solving length problems by turning one idea into multiple underpowered body slides.

## Bottom Line

The writing quality is high. The bank already has a strong hook engine, a distinctive scholarly voice, and several genuinely excellent carousels. The main work now is not inventing better premises. It is tightening bodies, lowering reader processing cost, and making closers turn more often from summary into image or implication.
