### calamus-far-smell
**Verdict: REVISE**

**Required changes:**

The hook now reads: "Two plants share the same marsh bed and look identical at any distance. One smells like nothing. One announces itself before you reach it. The smell is the only test."

"One smells like nothing" is factually wrong and contradicts the source.

Verification against NH 12.104–106 (workbench/pliny_nh_12_13.txt, lines 428–434):

At NH 12.104, Pliny introduces both calamus and the rush together: "calamus et iuncus odorati gignuntur" — *both* are described as aromatic ("odorati"). At NH 12.105, the contrast is: "nihil ergo a ceteris sui generis differunt aspectu, sed calamus praestanti odore statim e longinquo invitat" — they do not differ in appearance, but calamus invites you with a *praestanti odore* (outstanding/exceptional smell) from a distance. The passage contrasts the superiority of calamus's smell against the other plants of its genus; it does not say the rush is scentless. The rush is explicitly called "iuncus odoratus" (aromatic/scented rush) in the same sentence that introduces it. Its price — 5 denarii per pound against calamus at 1 — further confirms commercial value tied to scent.

The PM brief asked the writer to lead with the look-alike danger and set up the smell test as the distinguishing feature. That structure is correct and the rest of the hook delivers it. The single problem is the invented claim "One smells like nothing." The source says the rush also smells but that calamus smells *better* and *farther*.

Fix: replace "One smells like nothing. One announces itself before you reach it." with something that accurately represents the contrast — calamus has the stronger, farther-reaching smell; the rush is aromatic but does not announce itself the same way. For example: "One gives no warning from a distance. One announces itself before you reach it." Or more precisely: "Both are aromatic. Only one draws you from a distance." The existing closer ("a smell you can detect before you arrive") already handles this correctly — the hook just needs to match it.
