# WP13 Alchemy Reset: New Replacement Ideas

Goal: replace the failed concepts behind `maria-speaks-in-apparatus` and `drawn-up-dripped-fixed` with source-backed scenes from `workbench/sources/alchem.md` that a general reader can picture on first read.

Rule for this reset: prefer one visible action and one visible result. Avoid concept posts built around compressed technical labels.

## 1. Warm Ash Makes The Sulfur-Water Set

Premise: Maria gives a simple instruction sequence: add sulfur-water and a little gum, put it in warm ash, and the liquid sets.

Source scene in plain English: this is not theory or biography. It is a short workshop instruction about what to add, where to place it, and what the heat does to the liquid.

Source anchor: `alchem.md` lines 30-34.

Key Greek cues:
- `βάλλων ὕδωρ θείου καὶ κόμμι ὀλίγον`
- `θὲς ἐν θερμοσποδιᾷ`
- `τὸ ὕδωρ πήγνυσθαι`

Why it is audience-graspable: warm ash, gum, and liquid setting are all concrete. A reader can picture the step immediately without needing to learn alchemical vocabulary first.

Overlap risk: low. It is procedural like the current Maria post, but much more visible and much less abstract. It does not duplicate `copper-is-dyed`.

Verdict: `strong replacement candidate`

## 2. Leave It Three Days On The Double Plate

Premise: Maria gives another crisp instruction: make a double plate, put copper and gold on it, and leave it for three days until it bakes.

Source scene in plain English: the text moves from ingredients to arrangement and waiting time. The reader gets an object, metals placed on it, and a clear duration.

Source anchor: `alchem.md` lines 32-37.

Key Greek cues:
- `ποίει δίχυτον πέταλον`
- `Χαλκοῦ τοῦ ἡμῶν μέρος ἓν, χρυσοῦ μέρος ἓν`
- `ἔα νυχθήμερα γʹ`
- `ἕως ὀπτηθῇ`

Why it is audience-graspable: “double plate” and “leave it three days” are stronger handles than “apparatus” or “procedure.” The timing also gives the post a clean narrative spine.

Overlap risk: low to medium. It belongs to the same source cluster as idea 1, so the two should not both become full posts unless they are clearly separated by focus: one on setting the liquid, one on the waiting plate.

Verdict: `strong replacement candidate`

## 3. Sulfur-Water Climbs Through The Tube

Premise: Maria describes sulfur-water being sent upward through a pan and tube.

Source scene in plain English: the setup is simple enough to picture: a vessel below, a tube, and material moving upward through it.

Source anchor: `alchem.md` lines 171-173.

Key Greek cues:
- `Ὕδωρ θεῖον ληφθήσεται`
- `διὰ τῆς λωπάδος καὶ τοῦ σωλῆνος`
- `εἰς ὕψος ἀναπέμπεται`

Why it is audience-graspable: “through the tube and up” is understandable if kept visual.

Why it is still risky: this is the source cluster that generated the failed `drawn-up-dripped-fixed` concept. If written carelessly it slides back into sloganized process language.

Overlap risk: medium. It can blur into the existing failed abstraction if the draft starts naming processes instead of showing motion.

Verdict: `backup`

## 4. Burn The Shadow Out Of Copper

Premise: Maria describes copper being burned with sulfur, worked again and again, and stripped of its sulfurous “shadow” until it becomes shadowless.

Source scene in plain English: this is a strong transformation scene. The metal is burned, shaken or worked, repeated through the same treatment, and purified by driving something dark out of it.

Source anchor: `alchem.md` lines 367-378, with the same pattern repeated at lines 385-391.

Key Greek cues:
- `Ὁ χαλκὸς καεὶς θείῳ`
- `καὶ πολλάκις τὰ αὐτὰ παθῶν`
- `ἐκφυσᾶτε ἕως ἐκφύγωσιν ... τὰ θειώδη`
- `γίνεται χαλκὸς ἀσκίαστος`

Why it is audience-graspable: “burn the shadow out of copper” is vivid and physical. It gives the reader a before-and-after state instead of an abstract process name.

Overlap risk: low. It touches the same world as `copper-is-dyed`, but its focus is not dyeing language. It is on repeated firing, sulfur, and the disappearance of shadow.

Verdict: `strong replacement candidate`

## 5. Alteration Means Dyeing

Premise: the alchemical lexicon defines alteration itself as dyeing.

Source scene in plain English: this is not a scene so much as a clean lexical claim: change in this technical language is described as `βαφή`.

Source anchor: `alchem.md` lines 7703-7705.

Key Greek cues:
- `Ἀκαύστωσίς ἐστι λεύκωσις`
- `Ἀλλοίωσίς ἐστι βαφή`

Why it is audience-graspable: the line is short, surprising, and easy to explain.

Why it is limited: it is better as support than as a standalone replacement scene. On its own it risks becoming another glossary-style carousel.

Overlap risk: high. It overlaps directly with `copper-is-dyed`, which already carries the dye-language burden for this cluster.

Verdict: `reserve/reject`
