## `kerotakis-gentle-heat`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
The `kerotakis` is not presented here as a mystery box. It is a way of giving a batch gentle, managed ash heat instead of exposing it to a violent fire.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:30-37`
  - Maria says to add sulfur-water and a little gum, set it in warm ash, and elsewhere in the same cluster describes a double plate left for three days until it bakes.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:1865-1870`
  - The text defines the right heat plainly: not blazing fire, but gentle warm ash; this is what the `κηροτακίς` is.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.146.10ff.; Synes. Alchem., CAAG 2.60.21ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the `kerotakis` is tied to moderated heat rather than direct blast heat
- the texts care about how heat is delivered, not just that heat is present
- this belongs in the same broader workshop world as perfume setups that warm a batch without scorching it

Do not infer:

- that perfumers used the term `κηροτακίς`
- that this device is identical to the perfume-side doubled vessel
- that every ash-heated setup across trades is the same tool

Comparison note:

- compare in framing to `double-perfumers-vessel`
- the overlap is gentle, indirect heat and management of a delicate batch

Overlap to avoid:

- do not simply rewrite `double-perfumers-vessel` with Greek alchemical nouns

Drafting note:

- the hook should open on the surprise that the device is defined by mild ash heat, not fierce flame

## `tribikos-three-tubes`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
One alchemical rig is built from three beaten-copper tubes, a fitted copper body, clay-sealed joins, and glass receivers on the ends.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:686-700`
  - Zosimos describes the `τρίβικος`: three copper tubes, attached around the base, fixed at the joins, set over an earthen pan holding the sulfur material, with large glass jars placed on the tube ends.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:788-807`
  - A second version repeats the same tool-family in slightly different editorial form, confirming the rig rather than just mentioning it.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.236.3ff.; cf. Zos., Mertens, Les alchimistes grecs 4.1, lines 2ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the ancient workshop could involve complex multi-part rigs, not just loose pots and bowls
- sealing joins and attaching receiving vessels are central parts of the setup
- this belongs to the same broader craft world as perfume work that depends on vessel fit, closures, and controlled transfer

Do not infer:

- that the `tribikos` has a direct perfume-name equivalent in the current sources
- that perfumers used this exact three-tube configuration
- that the post is about abstract “distillation” rather than visible hardware

Comparison note:

- compare in framing to `breathing-jars` and `double-perfumers-vessel`
- the overlap is managed joins, containers fitted to one another, and movement of material through hardware

Overlap to avoid:

- do not let this collapse into a generic “ancient distillation” explainer

Drafting note:

- let the reader picture the rig piece by piece: three tubes, sealed joints, jars on the ends

## `watching-through-the-flask`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
This setup is built so the operator can suspend a vessel, seal the joins, place the plate beneath, and still see inside while the batch cooks.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:715-725`
  - The suspended sulfur setup uses a flask whose base is cut, a thin earthen vessel fitted into it, a plate placed below, sealed joins, and explicit visibility into the interior.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:842-853`
  - A parallel version repeats the arrangement as Maria’s furnace, again with the fitted flask, thin vessel, plate, and the ability to look inside.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.237.24ff.; cf. Zos., Mertens, Les alchimistes grecs 4.1, line 16ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the text cares about inspection and monitoring, not only heating
- this is a workshop of fitted containers, suspended pieces, and visible control
- the comparison to perfume tools lies in watching the batch and managing closures

Do not infer:

- exact perfume-tool identity
- a universal shop layout
- that visibility makes this “scientific” in a modern sense

Comparison note:

- compare in framing to `breathing-jars` and secondarily `double-perfumers-vessel`
- the overlap is monitoring, sealing, and deciding how much the vessel should be closed or visible

Overlap to avoid:

- do not let this become another vague “Maria gives instructions” post

Drafting note:

- the hook should open on cutting the bottom of the flask so you can fit the rest of the apparatus and still see inside
