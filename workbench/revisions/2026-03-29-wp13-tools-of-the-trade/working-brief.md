# Working Brief — WP13 Tools Pivot

Date: 2026-03-29

## Objective

Replace the obscure alchemical direction taken by `maria-speaks-in-apparatus` and `drawn-up-dripped-fixed` with a new `TOOLS OF THE TRADE` mini-batch built from concrete tool scenes in `workbench/sources/alchem.md`.

This pivot uses the lexical clue from `alchem.md` that `ἀλλοίωσις ἐστι βαφή` as a framing principle, not as a standalone post. The point is not to write another concept carousel about transformation. The point is to look for the hardware and handling that place alchemical work inside the same broader tool-world as perfumery and dyeing.

## Series target

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Batch size: 3 posts

These posts should speak of alchemical devices as often common instruments of the ergasteria of antiquity: fitted vessels, gentle heat, sealed joins, suspended pieces, receiving jars, and visible monitoring. The comparison should feel workshop-based, not doctrinal.

## Editorial direction

Lead with objects and actions the reader can picture immediately:

- warm ash
- double plate
- three copper tubes
- flask with a cut base
- thin receiving jar
- clay-sealed joins
- water rising through a tube

Keep the perfume comparison mostly in framing and closers. The body slides should stay source-first and scene-first.

## Claim posture

Allowed framing:

- alchemical tools belong to the same broader workshop world as perfume tools
- ancient ergasteria shared habits of heating, sealing, suspending, receiving, and watching materials
- some instruments or instrument-families are best understood as common workshop hardware rather than as isolated “alchemical” curiosities

Do not claim:

- every perfumer used the `κηροτακίς`
- every alchemical instrument has a direct perfume equivalent
- alchemy and perfumery are the same craft
- the comparison proves origin or dependence

## Tone rules

Required:

- concrete, visible, hand-level
- more Instagram than seminar
- comparison arrives after the object is visible
- hooks readable without Greek

Forbidden:

- aphoristic process slogans
- unexplained terms like `anaspasis`, `fixation`, `process logic`, `apparatus side`
- lines like `x matters`, `the point is`, `what this shows`
- abstract “proto-chemistry” summary language
