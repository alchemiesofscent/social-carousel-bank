# Editorial Review: 2026-03-17-wp9a

## Source Fidelity Check

**HARD FAIL on nero-poppaea-funeral.** The second body slide states: "Nero didn't cremate her -- that would have been Roman custom. He embalmed her body with spices and had it carried to the Mausoleum of Augustus." This claim is from Tacitus (Annals 16.6), not from Pliny NH 12. The provided source text (NH 12.82-83) says only that Nero burned more frankincense at Poppaea's funeral than Arabia's annual harvest. It says nothing about embalming, spices, or the Mausoleum of Augustus. The post cites "Pliny, NH 12.83" as its sole source.

**Additional fidelity flag on tus-supply-chain.** The draft conflates the Atramitae with the Minaei. NH 12.52 names the Atramitae as a pagus of the Sabaeans whose capital is Sabota. But the detail about "only three families" with hereditary harvesting rights who "call themselves sacri" and cannot "touch a woman or attend a funeral" belongs to the Minaei (NH 12.54: "hi primi commercium turis fecere... feruntque III non amplius esse familiarum"). The draft attributes these details to the Atramitae. This is a misattribution within the source.

**Additional fidelity flag on balsam-judea.** The second body slide says "The sap sold at 1,000 denarii per sextarius. The treasury resold it at 300." This reverses the market flow. NH 12.123 reads: "milibus denarium sextarii, empti vendente fisco trecenis denariis, veneunt" -- sextarii sell for thousands of denarii after having been bought from the fiscus at 300 denarii each. The fiscus sells cheaply; the dealers adulterate and mark up. The draft implies the sap started at 1,000 and the treasury discounted it to 300. The economics run the opposite direction.

All other posts (cinnamon-fables, piper-gold) pass the source fidelity gate.
