Date: 2026-03-25

- Keep the post outside WBS counting as an extension / one-off.

Conceptual spine:
Athenaeus preserves the titles of multiple Greek works devoted to perfumes and garlands. Most of those works are now lost. Theophrastus is the comparison case: perfume writing can also survive inside a larger technical investigation rather than as a clearly separate surviving perfume book.

Key passages:

Allowed claims:
- Athenaeus explicitly names titled works on perfumes and garlands.
- Philonides wrote `On Perfumes and Garlands`.
- Athenaeus says Apollodorus wrote a work of the same title and in the same words.
- Apollonius the Herophilean wrote `On Perfumes`.
- Hicesius is quoted from book 2 of `On Raw Materials` on perfume distinctions and uses.
- Theophrastus is quoted from `On Odours`.
- By Athenaeus' witness, perfume knowledge circulated in organized written form, not only as scattered remarks.
- Most of the named perfume-book witnesses here survive only through Athenaeus' quotations.
- Theophrastus should be used as a comparison case for a different survival path, not as a straightforward member of the lost-book roster.

Do not infer:
- That all these authors wrote only standalone perfume monographs of the same kind.
- That Hicesius' `On Raw Materials` was exclusively about perfume.
- That these are definitively the earliest perfume writers in all antiquity.
- That Theophrastus' `On Odours` straightforwardly survives as an isolated standalone perfume book.
