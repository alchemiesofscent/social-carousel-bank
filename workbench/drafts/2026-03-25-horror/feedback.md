**REVISE — one body slide issue:**

Body 2 repeats the hook's work more than it advances the argument.

> Quoted problem text: "Myrrh, frankincense, and saffron were temple and pharmacy staples. Here they anchor a recipe that also calls for baboon dung. The same ingredient that consecrates a temple appears in a recipe listing faeces, moon beetles, and ibis eggs alongside it."

Body 1 already establishes that the contrast is structural and not random. Body 2 then re-lists the same ingredients the hook already enumerated (myrrh, frankincense, saffron; baboon dung; moon beetles; ibis eggs) and restates the temple-vs.-recipe contrast Body 1 named. Apply the cover test: Body 1 frames the logic, the closer lands the implication — Body 2 is currently additive, not progressive.

What to change: Either cut Body 2 entirely, or replace its content with one move that Body 1 cannot make. One option is to use this slide to specify what "structural to the recipe's logic" actually means — i.e., what work the combination was believed to do (the luxury aromatics activate the polluting materials, or vice versa). If Coughlin's source makes that argument, this is the place for it. If not, cut the slide. The post is stronger as a three-slider.

**REVISE — hook final sentence:**

> Quoted problem text: "This was the timetable. It was written into a papyrus prologue as proof of a demonstration staged before the Emperor Hadrian."

The first two sentences of the hook are excellent — the timetable alone creates the gap. The third sentence ("This was the timetable.") is a fine pivot. But the fourth sentence does too much: it names the papyrus, names the prologue function, names the demonstration, and names Hadrian — all at once. The hook over-explains before the body can earn the context.

What to change: Cut "It was written into a papyrus prologue as proof of a demonstration staged before the Emperor Hadrian" and replace with something shorter that plants only one new fact. The batch spec itself models this: "That's the claim." Consider ending the hook at "This was the timetable" or adding a single line that opens rather than closes: e.g., "The papyrus records this as fact." Let Body 1 deliver Pachrates, Hadrian, and the institutional setting.

**REVISE — one foreign term in Body 1 prose needs translation:**

> Quoted problem text: "The recipe: take blood of a night owl and ζμυρνομέλαν, mix them, write with a new reed on clean paper."

`ζμυρνομέλαν` appears in the body prose without translation. The batch spec confirms the term means "myrrh ink," but Body 1 does not tell the reader this. The guidelines are explicit: one unfamiliar foreign term per body slide maximum, and foreign terms should be translated quickly. An Instagram reader encountering `ζμυρνομέλαν` mid-sentence without a gloss will stall.

What to change: Translate inline — e.g., "take blood of a night owl and ζμυρνομέλαν (myrrh ink), mix them" — or replace the Greek with the English and keep the Greek for the `script` field on the hook where it belongs. The second option is simpler and avoids a density stack on Body 1, which already introduces the night owl, the papyrus, and the falsifiable claim.
