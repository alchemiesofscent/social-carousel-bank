## `plato-smell-no-name`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
Plato treats smell as the least classifiable sense: odors happen during material change, but they do not form a stable catalog of named kinds.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/Plato.md:65-87`
  - `Timaeus` 66d-67a on smell having no `eidē`, arising in the interval between water and air, and collapsing into the pleasant/painful split.

Allowed claims:
- Plato says smell has no fixed species in the way other sensory domains do.
- He ties smell to states of material transition such as wetting, rotting, melting, and burning.
- He says all smells are either `smoke` or `mist` in the interval between air and water.
- The usable distinction he preserves is not a rich taxonomy but pleasant versus painful odor.

Do not infer:
- That Plato thinks smell is unimportant.
- That he denies smell is real.
- A modern chemistry claim about evaporation or molecules.

Overlap to avoid:
- `aristotle-nose`
- `smell-of-nothing`

Drafting note:
- The hook should foreground the category problem: smell is a sense, but Plato says it does not really have named kinds.

## `democritus-smell-shape`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
Through Theophrastus' report, Democritus explains smell materially: it is an effluence acting on a perceiver, governed by size, shape, order, and position rather than by hidden essence.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/Theophrastus.md:125-140`
  - Theophrastus sets up smell as `an effluence` and then pivots to Democritus and Plato as the thinkers who define the sensibles most fully.
- `workbench/sources/Theophrastus.md:410-413`
  - Theophrastus' criticism that Democritus leaves the nature of smell underspecified even after reducing it to a fine emanation from heavy bodies.

Allowed claims:
- Democritus is presented as giving a strongly material account of sensation.
- In Theophrastus' framing, smell belongs to the same explanatory world as shape, size, order, and position.
- Theophrastus is not fully satisfied with the account and says smell remains underexplained.
- The post can present the theory as a bold reduction, then show the critique.

Do not infer:
- That Theophrastus endorses Democritus without reservation.
- That the source gives a full atomist system of smell in modern technical detail.
- A direct perfume-industry application that the text does not make.

Overlap to avoid:
- `aristotle-nose`
- `last-added`

Drafting note:
- Keep the post on material explanation and critique, not on atomism in general.

## `stoic-smell-self-command`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
The Stoic slot should become an ethics-of-smell post: Marcus Aurelius treats foul smell as something to meet without anger, while Galen shows that smell still has real bodily and social consequences.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/marcus-aurelius.md:1-5`
  - `Meditations` 5.28 on not getting angry at a person with bad breath or rank armpits, because this is the kind of discharge such a body produces.
- `workbench/sources/galen-smell.md:1-9`
  - Galen's anecdotes about doctors who smell of wine, garlic, onions, or foul sweat and become intolerable to patients.

Allowed claims:
- Marcus Aurelius frames offensive smell as a test of rational self-command.
- He treats bodily odor as something with causes, not as grounds for rage.
- Galen shows that smell still matters in practice because it can distress patients and damage medical care.
- The combination supports an argument about smell as a moral and social problem, not just a sensory one.

Do not infer:
- Stoic physics of pneuma or mixture that these files do not supply.
- That Marcus Aurelius is writing a technical smell theory.
- That Galen is endorsing Stoic ethics.

Overlap to avoid:
- `perfume-and-soul`
- `gender-perfume`

Drafting note:
- Keep Marcus as the philosophical frame and Galen as the pressure test.
- Do not let the post become a generic hygiene post.

## `lucretius-scent-particles`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
Lucretius makes odor radically physical: smells stream off bodies, move through air, fit some animals better than others, and disperse before they travel as far as sound or sight.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/Lucretius.md:4-44`
  - `DRN` 4.673-705 on flowing waves of odor, species-specific sensitivity, and smell dispersing slowly through air.
- `workbench/sources/Lucretius.md:45-60`
  - Extension into differing sensory fit across animals, preserving the argument that perception depends on bodily constitution.

Allowed claims:
- Lucretius treats smell as a material outflow from bodies.
- Different creatures are differently tuned to different odors.
- Smell does not travel as far or as cleanly as sound because it disperses and cools.
- Broken, crushed, or heated things smell more strongly because odor is released from them.

Do not infer:
- That Lucretius gives a modern particle theory.
- That all animals smell the same world in the same way.
- A laboratory-science tone alien to the poem.

Overlap to avoid:
- `aristotle-nose`
- `smell-of-nothing`

Drafting note:
- The hook should lead with one concrete consequence, such as why crushed things smell stronger or why dogs lose the trail.
