# Editorial Review: 2026-03-24-wp10a

## Source Fidelity Check

**PASS with flags.** All major claims trace to the source texts. Two issues require correction:

1. **falernian-scale, body slide 2**: The draft says "His teacher Satyros mocked the old rule." The source (line 1213-1214) says Satyros was relaying one of Quintus's witticisms: "Kointou ton euprapelōn logōn hena kai tonde legontos" -- it was Quintus's joke, reported by Satyros. The draft drops Quintus entirely and attributes the mockery to Satyros alone. This is a misattribution -- not invented, but compressed in a way that loses the actual author of the quip.

2. **huckster-test, body slide 1**: The draft calls hēdychroion "a prepared sweet wine used in theriac." The Greek hēdychroion is a spiced/aromatic wine preparation used as an ingredient in theriac -- "sweet wine" follows the napkins translation but is slightly misleading. More importantly, the source says the doctor "sought hēdychroion from the perfumers, thinking it was a plant" (oiomenos einai botanēn). The draft reverses this: "asked for hēdychroion -- a prepared sweet wine used in theriac -- thinking it was a plant." The parenthetical correctly identifies what it is, but the sentence structure makes it sound like the doctor knew it was a prepared wine and still thought it was a plant, which muddles the anecdote. The point is that the doctor had no idea what hēdychroion was.

## Batch-Level Notes

1. **Closer convergence.** Three of five closers land on the same insight: Roman perfumers and doctors were limited, and the system was informal. galen-warehouse ("emperor's pantry"), huckster-test ("a favor"), and summer-ships ("last summer's basket") all end on "the market was unsophisticated." Vary the angle -- at least one closer should land on something other than the primitive state of ancient quality control.

2. **All five posts are THE MARKETPLACE.** That is expected per the batch spec, but it means structural variety within the batch is doing extra work. Currently four of five posts follow the same arc: set up a Roman market practice in slide 1, show how limited or corrupt it was in slide 2, reframe in the closer. falernian-scale is the exception (pattern + break). Consider whether huckster-test or blind-perfumers could use a different narrative arc -- e.g., a mystery arc or a character arc rather than the "here's how it worked, here's why it was flawed" revelation arc.

3. **The strongest posts in this batch are galen-warehouse and summer-ships.** Both have hooks that create genuine curiosity, body slides that deliver material the reader didn't expect, and closers that reframe rather than summarize. falernian-scale is close but needs a stronger closer. huckster-test and blind-perfumers both need structural tightening as detailed above.
