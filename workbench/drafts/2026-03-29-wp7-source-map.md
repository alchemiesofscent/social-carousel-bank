## `kerotakis-gentle-heat`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
The `kerotakis` is presented here less as a mysterious alchemical name than as a way of warming a batch gently in ash instead of blasting it with flame.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:30-37`
  - Maria says to add divine water and a little gum, set it in warm ash, and in the same cluster describes a double plate left for three days until it bakes.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:1865-1870`
  - The text states the heat plainly: not blazing fire, but mild warm ash; this is what the `κηροτακίς` is.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.146.10ff.; Synes. Alchem., CAAG 2.60.21ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the `kerotakis` is tied to moderated ash heat rather than direct fierce fire
- the texts care about how heat is delivered, not just that heat is present
- this belongs in the same broader workshop world as perfume setups that protect a delicate batch from scorching

Do not infer:

- that perfumers used the name `κηροτακίς`
- that this is identical to the perfume-side doubled vessel
- that every ash-heated setup across trades is the same tool

Overlap to avoid:

- `double-perfumers-vessel`

Drafting note:

- open on mild ash heat versus raging flame

## `tribikos-three-tubes`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
One alchemical rig is built from three beaten-copper tubes, a fitted copper body, sealed joins, and glass receiving jars on the ends.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:686-700`
  - Zosimos describes the `τρίβικος`: three copper tubes, attached around the base, fixed at the joins, set over an earthen pan holding the divine material, with large glass jars placed on the tube ends.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:788-807`
  - A second version confirms the same rig in slightly different editorial form.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.236.3ff.; cf. Zos., Mertens, Les alchimistes grecs 4.1, lines 2ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the ancient workshop could involve complex multi-part rigs, not just loose bowls and jars
- sealing joins and attaching receiving vessels are central to the setup
- this belongs to the same broader craft world as perfume work that depends on vessel fit, closures, and controlled transfer

Do not infer:

- that the `tribikos` has a direct perfume-name equivalent in the current sources
- that perfumers used this exact three-tube configuration
- that the post is really about abstract “distillation” rather than visible hardware

Overlap to avoid:

- `breathing-jars`
- `double-perfumers-vessel`

Drafting note:

- let the reader picture the rig piece by piece: three tubes, sealed joints, jars on the ends

## `watching-through-the-flask`

Series: `TOOLS OF THE TRADE`

Conceptual spine:
This setup is built so the worker can suspend a vessel, seal the joins, place the plate beneath, and still see inside while the batch cooks.

Key passages:

- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:715-725`
  - The suspended divine setup uses a flask whose base is cut, a thin earthen vessel fitted into it, a plate placed below, sealed joins, and explicit visibility into the interior.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:842-853`
  - A parallel version repeats the arrangement as Maria’s furnace, again with the fitted flask, thin vessel, plate, and the ability to look inside.

Public-facing citation target:

- `Zos. Alchem., CAAG 2.237.24ff.; cf. Zos., Mertens, Les alchimistes grecs 4.1, line 16ff.`

Allowed claims:

- the text cares about inspection and monitoring, not only heating
- this is a workshop of fitted containers, suspended pieces, and visible control
- the comparison to perfume tools lies in watching the batch and managing closures

Do not infer:

- exact perfume-tool identity
- a universal shop layout
- that visibility makes this “scientific” in a modern sense

Overlap to avoid:

- `breathing-jars`
- `double-perfumers-vessel`

Drafting note:

- open on cutting the bottom of the flask so the rest of the apparatus can be fitted and still watched
