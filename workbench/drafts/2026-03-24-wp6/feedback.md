# Editorial Review: 2026-03-24-wp6

## Source Fidelity Check

**PASS with caveats.** All five carousels trace their core claims to the Aetius source text. However, several carousels add interpretive or contextual claims not present in the source (trade route geography, "jeweler's units," Arab intermediaries). These are flagged below per carousel. No outright fabrication or misattribution found.

One source fidelity concern applies batch-wide: the claim that cloves, musk, ambergris, nutmeg, and camphor are "absent from Dioscorides, Theophrastus, Pliny, and Galen" is the batch spec's framing, not something the Aetius source text itself states. This is defensible as scholarly context, but the draft presents it as a verifiable textual claim. The writer should soften the framing or acknowledge this is a comparative observation, not something Aetius says.

## Batch-Level Notes

**Cross-batch variety summary (existing bank + this batch):**

The bank currently has 11 THE MARKETPLACE posts and 9 MATERIA posts. This batch adds 3 more MARKETPLACE and 2 MATERIA. The new posts draw on a different source author (Aetius vs. the bank's Dioscorides/Theophrastus/Galen focus), which provides good variety in source material. The late-antique / Byzantine setting is new to the account.

**Repeated structural move -- the "absent from classical sources" frame:**

Posts 2 (new-spices) and 5 (karyophyllon) both build their hooks and body slides around the same comparative-absence argument. This is the batch's most serious variety problem. Fix: keep the frame for one post (new-spices is the better vehicle as the survey/overview) and find a different hook for karyophyllon (e.g., start from the ubiquity side -- "One ingredient appears in nearly every recipe in a 6th-century collection. No classical author had ever heard of it." This reverses the direction and changes the reading experience).

**Trade-route geography as unsourced filler:**

Three posts (new-spices, church-incense, karyophyllon) use trade-route geography (Maluku Islands, Banda Islands, Arab intermediaries, Strait of Malacca) as body-slide content. The batch spec warns this is contextual, not textual. The writer is using it as a crutch to fill body slides. In each case, the Aetius text itself is more interesting than the geography. Lean into the recipes, the Greek terms, and the quantities. Let the reader infer the trade routes from the ingredients.

**Closer variety across the batch:**

- castor-linseed: number-closer ("twenty-three words") -- distinct
- new-spices: reframe-closer ("the ships arrived") -- distinct
- moschos: principle-closer ("you've found the expensive part") -- distinct
- church-incense: image-closer ("smelled of the Maluku Islands") -- distinct
- karyophyllon: parallel-closer ("One ingredient... One ingredient...") -- distinct

Good variety in closer shapes. No repeated templates.

**Strongest post in the batch:** castor-linseed. Tight spine, two body slides that both earn their place, a specific Greek quotation that anchors the entire post, and a closer that lands with a concrete number. This is the model the other four should aim for.

**Weakest post in the batch:** karyophyllon. Duplicates new-spices in hook, body, and arc. Body slide 2 is entirely unsourced geography. Needs the most revision.

**Overall verdict:** 2 PASS (castor-linseed, moschos), 3 REVISE (new-spices, church-incense, karyophyllon). The batch has strong raw material but relies too heavily on trade-route geography that the source text does not support. The fix in every case is the same: trust the Aetius text more and the contextual framing less.
