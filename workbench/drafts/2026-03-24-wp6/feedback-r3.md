**Transliteration note:** The source has "Κρείττων" (Kreitton), but the draft writes "Kritton." These are variant spellings of the same name across manuscript traditions, so this is not a hard fail. However, if accuracy matters to the account, consider changing to "Kreitton" to match this edition.

**Translation note:** The draft translates "ΕΜΒΟΛΑΡΧΟΥ" as "harbor master." The embolarchos was more precisely the superintendent of the embolos, a covered commercial arcade in Byzantine cities -- not a harbor official. This is a real but minor inaccuracy. Consider "arcade superintendent" or simply leaving the Greek title untranslated with a gloss like "a commercial official." Alternatively, a short phrase like "a market official" would be more defensible than "harbor master." Not a hard fail, but worth correcting since the post is specifically about identifying these people.

| Dimension | Score | Verdict |

**Issues to address:**

> Quoted problem text: "Most ancient recipes\nare anonymous.\nAetius names names."
> What to change: This duplicates the hook template from mendesian-paul ("Most ancient perfumes\nare boiled.\nThis one was stirred\nfor sixty days.") and melinon in the bank ("Most ancient perfumes\nsmell like resins, flowers, or spices. / This one smells like fruit."). Rewrite the hook to avoid the "Most X are Y / This one Z" structure. The strongest material is actually in the subText -- "A perfumer. A governor. A lady. A harbor master. And himself." -- which is a concrete, surprising list. Consider leading with the list or with the specific surprise that a governor and a lady had personal perfume recipes in a medical encyclopedia.

> Quoted problem text: "He also cites Kamesandreas for the Sicyonian oil recipe and quotes Kritton on the properties of amarakinon. Archigenes appears with a note on fenugreek."
> What to change: This reads as a name-dump. Three names in rapid succession with minimal context. The reader cannot distinguish these figures or understand why they matter. Either cut one or two names and give the remaining one a concrete detail, or restructure so each name does specific work in the argument. The post's spine is about the *types* of people who appear (practitioners vs. patrons) -- so each name should illustrate a type, not just accumulate. Compare how the Book 16 slide handles its names: each one comes with a title that tells you who they are (governor, lady, harbor master).

> Quoted problem text: "the harbor master"
> What to change: "ΕΜΒΟΛΑΡΧΟΥ" refers to the superintendent of a commercial arcade (embolos), not a harbor master. Use "market official" or "arcade superintendent," or leave the Greek title and gloss it.

## Summary

The carousel has a strong spine and a genuinely good closer. The main issues are (1) the repeated hook template, which needs a structural rewrite, (2) the name-catalog feel of body slide 1, and (3) the minor "harbor master" inaccuracy. Fix those three and this is ready to pass.
