## Notes for post 6 (susinum)

**Origin story — the lost Egyptian connection:** Paul of Aegina (7.20.7) writes: "Κρίνινον, οἱ δὲ σούσινον διὰ τὸ ἐν Σούσοις ἴσως εὑρῆσθαι" — "Krininon, also called sousinom because it was perhaps found in Susa." But Athenaeus (XII 513f) records that σοῦσον is the Persian word for κρίνον (lily). The real etymology is transparent: sousinom = lily perfume. The connection to the Egyptian lily perfume zeshen — one of the oldest perfumes in the ancient world — was already lost by Paul's time. He guesses "Susa" as an origin city instead.

**The recipe itself (Paul 7.20.8, "compound susinum"):** 3 sextarii of oil, 5 lbs aromatic calamus, 5 drachms myrrh, 3 lbs cardamom, 10 lbs lily petals (trimmed and cooled), 3 drachms cinnamon, 5 drachms Cilician saffron. Three separate infusions over many days. The lily petals are replaced three times. Final addition: cinnamon, or substitute with cassia (double the amount) or carpesius.

**Key detail:** Paul classifies susinum as "moderately warm, fine-particled, soothing, and digestive" (7.20.3) — grouped with amarakinon, irinon, and krokinon. It had medical applications, not just cosmetic.

## Notes for post 7 (mendesian)

**Origin story — the goat of Mendes:** Paul (7.20.31) writes: "Εἴρηται μὲν διὰ τὸ ἐν Αἰγύπτῳ εὑρῆσθαι, ἔνθα καὶ ὁ μένδης τρέφεται" — "It is called [Mendesian] because it was found in Egypt, where the mendes (goat) is raised." Mendes was an Egyptian city famous for its sacred goat cult.

**The recipe (Paul 7.20.31):** Balaninos oil 10 lbs, myrrh 3 drachms, cassia 3 drachms, syrinx 3 drachms, terebinth 1 lb, cinnamon 3 drachms. **Unique process:** NOT boiled. Dry ingredients stirred for 60 days. Then terebinth melted into a portion of the oil and added. Stirred another 7 days.

**Aetius entry 126 echoes:** "Mendesian is the most softening and relaxing of bodies, and promotes suppuration. Composed of myrobalanos oil, myrrh, cassia, and resin. Some also add cinnamon after the astringent stage."

**Key detail:** Paul (7.20.2) notes that some perfumes "do not admit boiling at all, but are only mixed — like the Mendesian." This cold-processing is the defining feature.

**Cross-reference from napkins.md line 545:** "Mendesium was best supplied by Egypt."

## Notes for post 8 (cleopatra-regime)

**Framing:** The source (napkins.md:1205-1301) explicitly raises the question: "Maybe Cleopatra wrote them, or maybe they were marketing gimmicks by booksellers." The post should lean into this tension — these are recipes *attributed to* Cleopatra, preserved in medical compilations. Whether she wrote them is unknowable, but the attribution itself tells us something about ancient marketing.

**Recipe 1 — Cleopatra's Sweet-Smelling Soap (Aetius 8.6):**
"Ἄλλο σμῆγμα Κλεοπάτρας βασιλίσσης πολυτελὲς εὐῶδες" — "Another soap, Queen Cleopatra's, very expensive and fragrant."
Ingredients: 1 oz each of costus, Troglodytic myrrh, iris, spikenard, Nepal cardamom, cassia leaves, flowers of camel grass; 4 lbs of perfume-nut (myrobalanos); 2 lbs foam of soda. "Grind, sift, and use. Works on the whole body."

**Recipe 2 — Anti-Dandruff Shampoo (Galen, Comp. Med. XII 492):**
Galen introduces this as: "τὰ τῇ Κλεοπάτρᾳ πρὸς ἀχῶρας γεγραμμένα... κατὰ τὴν ἐκείνης αὐτῆς λέξιν" — "the things Cleopatra wrote against dandruff... in her very own words."
Boiled fenugreek steeped in beet juice, wash head, then myrtle paste with wine and oil, beet leaves on top.

**Recipe 3 — Face Brightener (Aetius 8.6):**
"Σμῆγμα λαμπρυντικὸν προσώπου" — "Soap for brightening the face."
4 drachms each frankincense, foam of soda, gum arabic; 40 peeled almonds; 24 drachms wheat flour; 12 drachms bean flour. Mix with egg white, form into small balls. Use with water in the bath.

**The Galen attribution is the most interesting:** He claims to quote "in her very own words." This is either a genuine textual transmission or a very old marketing attribution. Either way, Galen — not a credulous author — treats it as real.
