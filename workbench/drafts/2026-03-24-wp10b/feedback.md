**Source fidelity issues (REVISE required):**

> Quoted problem text: "To the butcher: 'How much for a knuckle of pork?'"
> What to change: The Greek says mageirion (cook-shop), not butcher. The akrokolion is a trotter/knuckle bought at a cook-shop, not a butchery. Change "butcher" to "cook-shop" or "the cooked-food stall."

> Quoted problem text (omission): The draft skips the wool/sheep stop entirely. The Greek (lines 172-174) has Diogenes leading the man to "ta eria ta malaka" (soft wool) and asking the price of a sheep -- one mina. The man cries out again. This is a significant narrative beat: TWO expensive items at a mina each (perfume AND sheep) before the cheap items. Omitting it weakens the source's structure. Add the wool/sheep stop or acknowledge the cut explicitly.

> Quoted problem text: "A rich man complained"
> What to change: The Greek says "ton polytele" -- "the extravagant/luxurious man," not "rich." The distinction matters for a Cynic lesson about lifestyle, not wealth. Change to "an extravagant man" or "a man who lived expensively."

> Quoted problem text: "Diogenes took him by the arm"
> What to change: Minor embellishment. The Greek says "labon auton egen" -- "taking him, he led him." No arm is mentioned. Drop "by the arm."

**Source fidelity issue (REVISE required):**

> Quoted problem text: "Liquids require silver, glass, or horn vessels -- never porous clay."
> What to change: The Greek (line 1149-1150) says "ostrakine de he me araia euthetos" -- pottery that is NOT porous IS suitable. Dioscorides also allows boxwood vessels for liquids ("xylinon de hosa ek pyxou kataskeuazetai"). The draft inverts the source: Dioscorides says non-porous clay works fine; the draft says "never porous clay," which implies all clay is excluded. Correct to something like: "Liquids in silver, glass, horn, or dense pottery -- never porous clay. Wooden vessels only if boxwood."

**Structural revision:**

> Cut body slide 2 or merge its key content into slide 3. The strongest version of this post is: hook (three-year expiration) -> body 1 (harvest conditions: weather, terrain) -> body 2 (storage as material science: what container for what substance) -> closer. The timing content (when to pick flowers vs. seeds vs. roots) is interesting but belongs in a separate post or can be reduced to one sentence in the storage slide: "Pick at the right moment, store in the right vessel."

**Minor notes (not blocking PASS):**

> Quoted problem text: "built their shops upstairs"
> Note: The Greek says "seek" (zethousi) upper-story rooms, not "built." Consider "chose upper rooms" or "kept their shops upstairs" for closer fidelity.

> Quoted problem text: "Not just dim. Aggressively dark."
> Note: Editorializing beyond the source. The Greek says "hoti malista paliskious" -- "as shaded as possible." The interpretation is defensible but "aggressively dark" pushes into territory the source doesn't occupy. Consider "as deeply shaded as possible" to stay closer.

> Quoted problem text: "The scent survives intact, waiting to return."
> Note: "Waiting to return" is not in Theophrastus. The source says cold does not completely remove potency. The personification is evocative but adds a claim (that scent returns) the source only implies. Consider "The scent survives intact beneath the cold" or similar.
