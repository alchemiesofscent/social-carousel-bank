### hicesius-drinking-perfumes -- PASS

Full re-review of replacement carousel:

| Dimension | Score | Verdict |
|-----------|-------|---------|

| Cross-Post Variety | ✅ | No overlap with perfume-map (regional geography), body-perfume-map (body-part mapping), or any existing bank entry. The "sommelier's guide" angle is new. Catalog/list structure is distinct from the other carousels in this batch. |

**Source fidelity:** All Hicesius claims verified against par. 39, lines 1501-1511.

- Rose suitable for drinking: "rhodinon men pros poton epitedeion" -- confirmed.
- Myrtle and quince approved: "eti de mursinon, melinon" -- confirmed.
- Quince good for stomach and useful for lethargy: "touto d' estin kai eustomachon kai lethargikois chresimon" -- confirmed.
- Dropwort/vine-blossom keeps the mind clear: "to d' oinanthinon eustomachon on kai ten dianoian aparapodiston phulassei" -- confirmed. (Note: the draft translates oinanthinon as "dropwort." The term more commonly refers to vine-blossom perfume. This is a minor translation choice, not a fidelity issue, but if precision matters, "vine-blossom" is safer.)
- Saffron without too much myrrh: "krokinon to choris smurnes polles" -- confirmed.
- Stakte approved: "he stakte de epitēdeios pros poton" -- confirmed.
- Fenugreek "sweet and delicate": "to de telinon kai gluku esti kai hapalon" -- confirmed.
- Gillyflower fragrant and good for digestion: "to de leukoinon kai euodes kai sphodra peptikon" -- confirmed. (The draft says "extremely good for digestion" -- sphodra peptikon is literally "very digestive," so this is accurate.)
- Chrimata vs. aleimmata distinction: "ton muron, phesin, ha men estin chrimata, ha d' aleimmata" -- confirmed. The draft renders this as "perfumes you pour on and perfumes you rub in," which is slightly loose (chrimata are anointings, not pourings), but acceptable for the medium.

No revisions needed.
