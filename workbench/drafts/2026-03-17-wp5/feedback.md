## Batch-Level Notes

**Structural repetition across THE RECIPE posts (4 of 6):** kyphi-plutarch, kyphi-edfu, kyphi-damocrates, and kyphi-dioscorides all follow the same arc: hook introduces a source, body slides catalog ingredients or process, closer reframes the source's perspective. The closers in particular converge on a single move — contrasting two authors' approaches. kyphi-edfu's closer ("Plutarch gave you cosmology. / Edfu gave you accounting.") and kyphi-dioscorides' closer ("Plutarch heard the music. / Dioscorides counted the grams.") are structurally and tonally identical. This needs breaking up.

**Closer shape concentration:** Five of six closers use a contrast-reframe (author A vs. author B, or sacred vs. clinical). Only kyphi-medicine uses a list-closer. The batch needs at least two different closer shapes among the RECIPE posts.

**Hook variety is better than the closers:** The hooks genuinely differ in entry angle (number + ritual, accounting precision, poetry form, stripped-down clinical tone, sacred/medical crossover, three-part cosmological scheme). This is a strength.

### kyphi-solar-lunar — REVISE

| Dimension | Score | Verdict |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Conceptual Spine | ✅ | Strong single idea: simple incense for a simple sun, compound incense for a compound night. |
| Hook | ⚠️ | "Resin belongs to the sun. / Kyphi belongs to the night." is clean but reads more like a thesis statement than a scroll-stopper. For the marquee post, this needs more surprise. |
| Body Slide Economy | ❌ | Three body slides. Slide 3 (Oribasius seleniakon) is genuinely interesting but feels bolted on — it introduces a new source and a new recipe without connecting it back to the solar-lunar cosmology. Cover test: remove slide 3 and the post still works. |
| Hook-to-Body Payoff | ✅ | The Plutarch material in slides 1-2 beautifully develops the hook's claim. The Pindar quote ("through a deserted aether") is excellent. |
| Closer | ✅ | "The sun gets two ingredients. / The night gets sixteen." is sharp and earned. |
| Cross-Post Variety | ✅ | This post's cosmological arc is distinct from the recipe-catalog arcs of the other posts. |

> Quoted problem text (hook): "Resin belongs to the sun. / Kyphi belongs to the night."
> What to change: As the marquee post, this hook needs more gap. The current version states a fact; it should create a question. Consider leading with the surprising practical detail — that Egyptians changed incense three times a day — before delivering the cosmological explanation.

> Quoted problem text (body slide 3): "The most surprising medical use comes from... The medical tradition echoes this. Oribasius records a variant called kyphi seleniakon — lunar kyphi. Twenty-four ingredients including bdellium, elecampane, mastic, juniper, saffron-crocus, aspalathus, costus, myrrh, styrax, dried roses, pine nuts, raisins, figs, dates, honey, and fragrant wine."
> What to change: This slide breaks the spine. The post is about Plutarch's cosmological scheme (sun = simple, night = compound). The Oribasius seleniakon is a separate discovery that deserves its own post — it could anchor a future PHARMAKON or DEAD WORDS entry. Cut it from this post. Two body slides (dawn/noon + nightfall) are tighter and more powerful.

**Source fidelity: REVISE.** The seleniakon ingredient list includes "saffron-crocus" — but the Oribasius source (kyphi.md line 834) does not list krokos/saffron among the seleniakon ingredients. The 36-ingredient kyphi recipes in Aetius (kyphi.md lines 93-95, 114-115) do include krokos, but the seleniakon recipe does not. This is a misattribution. Remove "saffron-crocus" from the seleniakon list or flag it with a different source.
