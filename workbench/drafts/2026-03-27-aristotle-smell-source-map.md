## `smell-middle-sense`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
Aristotle treats smell as the awkward middle sense: tied to nutritive and tactile life, yet also operating through a medium like the distance senses.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:8-15`
  - Smell works in both air and water; the smellable is not just a function of the transparent.
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:151-156`
  - Smell is `middle` between touch/taste and sight/hearing.
  - The smellable is common to both groups.
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:156`
  - Smell is compared to a kind of staining or washing of dryness in moisture.

Allowed claims:
- Aristotle says smell operates in both air and water.
- He places smell between touch/taste and sight/hearing.
- He treats smell as sharing features with both tactile and distance perception.
- Smell becomes a category problem inside his theory of the senses.

Do not infer:
- That Aristotle simply equates smell with vision.
- That he rejects all material transmission or bodily process.
- That this chapter gives a modern theory of olfactory chemistry.

Overlap to avoid:
- `aristotle-nose`
- the flower/brain-health argument already used from `On Sense 5`

## `animal-smell-distance`

Series: `THE NOSE KNOWS`

Conceptual spine:
Aristotle assumes many animals detect odors from far away, but he refuses to make all creatures smell by one identical bodily mechanism.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:13-15`
  - Fish and shell-creatures show that smell is not confined to breathing in air.
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:122-124`
  - Fish, insects, bees, ants, purple-shell creatures, and other animals detect food by smell from far away.
- `workbench/sources/arist-sense-5.md:129-134`
  - Aristotle asks how non-breathing creatures smell if smelling in breathing animals depends on respiration.
  - He compares the difference to animals with and without eyelids.

Allowed claims:
- Aristotle says many animals detect food-smells at a distance.
- He uses fish, insects, bees, ants, and sea creatures as examples.
- He distinguishes between breathing and non-breathing animals in the way smell works.
- The eyelid analogy can be used to show that olfactory apparatus differs across species.

Do not infer:
- That Aristotle offers a complete zoological taxonomy of olfaction.
- That all animals inhabit the same smell-world in the same way.
- That the vision analogy means smell is just another form of seeing.

Overlap to avoid:
- `aristotle-nose`
- `lucretius-scent-particles`
- generic "animals can smell better than humans" framing
