Required approach:
- Use the source map as the drafting dossier.
- Keep one decisive claim per post.
- Start from the strange claim or pressure point, not from author introduction.
- Cash out abstract theory through a bodily or sensory consequence as early as possible.
- Preserve uncertainty where the source is thin, but do not let uncertainty become the whole post.

## What to emphasize

- Smell as a problem of classification, not just pleasantness.
- Material explanations of odor: emission, transition, fit, dispersal.
- The fact that philosophical smell theories still stay close to bodies.
- One concrete anchor per post: smoke/mist, atomic shape, bad breath, crushed things smelling stronger.

## What to avoid

- Generic hooks like "Ancient philosophers also thought about smell."
- Unsupported Stoic physics of pneuma or mixing of bodies.
- Expository author-intro slides that delay the argument.
- Long quote-heavy body slides.
- Modern neuroscience wording.

## Editorial constraints

- Body slides must cash out the hook rather than surround it with setup.
- Use the lightest language that preserves the conceptual spine.
- Closers should reframe the argument through bodily, social, or epistemic stakes.
