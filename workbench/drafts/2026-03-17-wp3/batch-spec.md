## WBS alignment notes
- **Post 1** (euodia/dysōdēs) maps directly to WBS topic 1 (euōdes/dysōdes). Source covers both terms well.
- **Post 2** (plēktikon) replaces WBS topic 2 (drimys). The term drimys ("sharp") does not appear in the source text. Plēktikon ("striking/pungent") occupies the same conceptual slot — an aggressive, penetrating scent quality — and is richly attested in the source with examples and pharmacological context.
- **Post 3** (baryosmon/brōmōdēs) expands WBS topic 3 (barys). The source uses the compound baryosmon rather than the simple adjective barys, and pairs it naturally with brōmōdēs ("stinking"), which adds a second negative scent category absent from the WBS plan.
- **Post 4** (-izō verbs + purification) reworks WBS topic 4 (adulteration vocabulary). The source does not contain a dedicated adulteration section (that material lives in Diosc. 1.1–1.8, not yet transcribed). However, the -izō comparative verbs are Dioscorides' primary tool for detecting fakes ("this should smell of nard, not castoreum"), and the purification vocabulary (apoballō tēn osmēn) shows how bad smells were removed from raw materials. Together these cover the adulteration angle from the olfactory side.
- **Post 5** (olfactory fatigue / breathing scent / olfaction mechanics) replaces WBS topic 5 (styptikos). The term styptikos ("astringent") does not appear in the source text. The source instead contains three rich, closely related concepts about the physical mechanics of smell — olfactory fatigue (plērounta tēn osphrēsin), active "breathing" scent (pneon/peripneonta), and smelling as a medical act (osphrantos) — which together form a coherent post on how Dioscorides understood the nose as a pharmacological instrument.
