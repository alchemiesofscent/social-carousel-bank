# Editorial Review: 2026-03-17-wp9b

## Source Fidelity Check
**PASS**

All three carousels trace their claims to Pliny, Natural History Book XIII. Specific Latin quotations verified against the source text (NH 13.1-3 for post 1; 13.18 for post 2; 13.19-20 for post 3). The ingredient count of 27 for the regale unguentum is defensible (calamus and iuncus counted separately via -que). One soft note: post 1's hook says perfume "was invented by greed," but the Latin (iuvitque luxuria) is closer to "luxury delighted in mixing them all" -- luxuria is excess/indulgence, not strictly greed. This is editorial license, not a factual error, so it does not trigger a hard fail.

### pliny-royal-perfume -- REVISE

| Dimension | Score | Verdict |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Conceptual Spine | ⚠️ | Two ideas compete: (1) the astonishing complexity of the Parthian royal perfume, and (2) Rome's inability to grow any of its ingredients. Neither is fully developed. |
| Hook | ✅ | "The Parthian kings had a personal perfume. It had 27 ingredients." Specific, surprising, scroll-stopping. |
| Body Slide Economy | ⚠️ | Body slide 1 is overloaded -- it tries to deliver the full ingredient list AND the quote about Parthian kings, reading more like a catalog than an argument. The ingredient list is impressive as spectacle but doesn't build toward slide 2's editorial point. |
| Hook-to-Body Payoff | ⚠️ | The hook promises a royal perfume with 27 ingredients. Body 1 delivers the list (restating the hook's promise rather than exceeding it). Body 2 pivots to a different idea (Rome can't grow any of this). The payoff is split between two directions. |
| Closer | ✅ | "Rome conquered everything except the plants it needed to smell like a king." Vivid, tight, lands well. |
| Cross-Post Variety | ✅ | Distinct from mendesian-recipe (Dioscorides, 4 ingredients, brevity as theme) and from the other RECIPE posts (all Dioscorides-based process descriptions). This is the only RECIPE post built around geopolitical irony. |

**REVISE:**
> Quoted problem text: "The recipe reads like a trade map: myrobalanos, costus, amomum, cinnamon, cardamom, spikenard, myrrh, cassia, styrax, labdanum, opobalsam, calamus, oenanthe, malobathrum, saffron, marjoram, lotus, honey, wine. And more."
> What to change: The ingredient list dominates body slide 1 and reads as a wall of nouns. It fulfills the hook's promise (27 ingredients) but doesn't advance the argument toward body 2's trade-dependency point. Two options: (a) Cut the full list to 5-6 representative ingredients spanning geographically distinct origins ("costus from India, cinnamon from East Africa, spikenard from the Himalayas, opobalsam from Judea...") so the list itself becomes the trade map and sets up slide 2's punchline. (b) Merge the Parthian-kings quote and a shortened list into one leaner body slide, giving more room for the trade-dependency argument. Either way, the spine should commit: this post is about Rome's dependence on foreign aromatics, seen through the regale unguentum. Make the list serve that idea.

| Dimension | Score | Verdict |

| Body Slide Economy | ⚠️ | Body slide 2 is overloaded. It packs in alabaster jars, oil thickness, sunlight, lead vessels, shade, the hand-inversion test, AND the aging rule. | Hook-to-Body Payoff | ✅ | The hook promises a paradox (perfume's ephemerality). Body 1 delivers the comparison to pearls and clothing -- genuinely surprising material. Body 2 delivers the obsessive cataloging. The payoff works despite body 2's density. |
| Closer | ✅ | "He documents the science of preserving something he thinks no one should want." Lands the spine perfectly. Reframes without summarizing. |
| Cross-Post Variety | ⚠️ | The existing shelf-life post (Theophrastus, On Odours 38-41) covers similar ground: perfume longevity, alabaster storage, lead vessels, the same basic topic. The angle here is different (Pliny's moral contradiction vs. Theophrastus's empirical observations), but the factual overlap is real -- both mention alabaster, both mention aging improving perfume. **REVISE:**
> Quoted problem text: "Perfumes keep best in alabaster jars. Oil preserves scent longer when it's thicker. Sunlight destroys them — store in lead vessels, in shade. Test by inverting the hand: the fleshy part gives off heat that corrupts the sample. And like wine, unguenta vetustate meliora — 'perfumes improve with age.'"
> What to change: Body slide 2 tries to pack in every preservation detail Pliny mentions. Pick the 2-3 most vivid and cut the rest. The hand-inversion test is the most distinctive detail (it's not in the Theophrastus shelf-life post and it's a concrete sensory image). Lead the slide with that. Drop the alabaster and lead-vessel details -- they overlap with the existing shelf-life post and aren't necessary for the spine. The aging note ("unguenta vetustate meliora") could move to body 1 as a pivot: perfume dies in its own hour... and yet, like wine, improves with age. That would sharpen the contradiction the post is built around.
