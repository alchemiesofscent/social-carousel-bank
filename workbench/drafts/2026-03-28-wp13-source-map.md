## `rhopos-and-the-perfumer`

Series: `THE TRANSMUTATION`

Conceptual spine:
Perfumers do not just sit next to dyers and painters socially. The lexica and profession lists put them in the same working world of mixed color-materials, scents, and shop compounds.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:17-19`
  - Ptolemy groups work in `μύρα`, `χρώματα`, `βαφαί`, and `ἀρώματα`, then lists perfumers, painters, dyers, and related trades together.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:21-23`
  - Philo couples `βαφική` and `μυρεψική` as neighboring arts of sensory manipulation.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:29-35`
  - `ῥῶπος` is defined as mixed compounds/pigments used by dyers, painters, and perfumers.
- `workbench/napkins.md:1149`
  - Criton is reported as writing about perfumes in garments and fragrant dyes for clothing; use only as supporting evidence of overlap in practice.

Allowed claims:
- Ancient texts explicitly cluster perfumers with dyers and painters.
- Shared mixed materials, not just shared luxury status, tie these crafts together.
- The perfume seller can appear in the lexical world of compounds and pigments.

Do not infer:
- A unified guild, single workshop type, or identical profession.
- That every perfume seller also dyed cloth or painted.
- That Criton is alchemical evidence.

Overlap to avoid:
- `perfume-shops`
- `perfumers-shade`
- `perfume-colors`

Drafting note:
- Lead with the surprise that a perfume seller can show up inside a color-compound word.

## `stypsis-before-scent`

Series: `THE TRANSMUTATION`

Conceptual spine:
Before perfume smells right, the oil gets worked on. The sources describe that early handling with the same technical language used for preparing material for dye.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:37-39`
  - Galen describes perfumers giving the oil a preliminary `stypsis` before adding roses.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:45-50`
  - Pliny divides perfume into wet and dry parts and names `stymmata` as a formal component.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:77-79`
  - Theophrastus says perfumers pre-treat oil so it takes scent better, `ὥσπερ τὰ ἔρια εἰς τὴν βαφήν`.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:136-148`
  - The recipe tables place perfume `στῦψις` and dye `στῦψις καὶ βαφή` in parallel technical formats.

Allowed claims:
- `στῦψις` is a real technical bridge between perfumery and dyeing.
- The sources care about making oil able to receive scent, not just naming ingredients.
- Perfume and dye procedures can share preparatory structure without being identical crafts.

Do not infer:
- That `στῦψις` always means “thickening” and nothing else.
- That perfume and dye recipes are interchangeable.
- That every occurrence of `stymmata` or `stypsis` carries the same narrow meaning.

Overlap to avoid:
- `rhodinon-recipe`
- `sousinon-recipe`
- `rhodinon-simplicity`

Drafting note:
- Keep the post procedural: wash, boil, soak, prepare, then scent.

## `maria-speaks-in-apparatus`

Series: `THE TRANSMUTATION`

Conceptual spine:
Maria enters the corpus not as a legend first, but as a voice giving directions: sulfur-water, gum, warm ash, the plate of the kerotakis, three days of controlled exposure.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:21-37`
  - Water remains below in the `κηροτακίς`; Maria gives instructions with sulfur-water, gum, warm ash, the `πέταλον`, and a double plate.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:171-188`
  - Maria is cited on sulfur-water being sent upward through pan and tube, then the process continues through prism or `κηροτακίς`.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:193-204`
  - The same passage ties vessel choice, roasting, whitening, and controlled fire to the operation.

Allowed claims:
- Maria appears in the local corpus as a named technical authority.
- Her instructions are apparatus-heavy and heat-managed.
- `κηροτακίς` and `πέταλον` belong to the practical language of setup, not just symbolic allegory.

Do not infer:
- A full biography of Maria the Jewess.
- That Maria is here a perfume author.
- That perfumers directly used the exact same setup.

Overlap to avoid:
- `double-perfumers-vessel`
- `organon-not-cloth`
- `breathing-jars`

Drafting note:
- Open on the command voice, not on “Maria matters.”

## `drawn-up-dripped-fixed`

Series: `THE TRANSMUTATION`

Conceptual spine:
The local alchemical corpus does support a real process sequence: material is drawn up through the apparatus, drips or distills out, and is then fixed.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:149-151`
  - Democritus is cited on lime-water dripping `διὰ τοῦ ῥυτοῦ στάζον, ἢ δι' ὑλιστῆρος`.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:165-173`
  - Waters rise again through an `ὄργανον`; Maria says sulfur-water is sent upward through pan and tube `εἰς ὕψος ἀναπέμπεται`.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:2448-2464`
  - A later recipe says `ἀνάσπα διὰ τοῦ ὀργάνου`, then set the mixture in the apparatus `εἰς ἀπόσταξιν τέχνης`, seal it tightly, and let it drip out.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:3093-3120`
  - Zosimos explicitly clusters `ἀνασπάσεως` and `ἀποστάζειν`, then says the end of the art lies in the apparatus that draws up copper and in its `πήξις`.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:30-34`
  - Maria already uses `πήγνυται` for the fixing/solidifying side of the process.

Allowed claims:
- The local corpus supports a linked sequence of drawing up, dripping/distilling, and fixing.
- This is process language tied to apparatus use, not just metaphor.
- `πήξις` is part of the same technical conversation as rising and dripping.

Do not infer:
- Clean one-to-one equivalence with later laboratory categories.
- That every passage uses the same apparatus shape.
- A tidy textbook definition of “sublimation” or “distillation” that the Greek texts themselves do not give.

Overlap to avoid:
- `from the workshop` process posts
- `maria-speaks-in-apparatus`

Drafting note:
- Keep the slide sequence visual: up, drip, set.

## `copper-is-dyed`

Series: `THE TRANSMUTATION`

Conceptual spine:
The alchemical texts keep describing metallic change with dye-language. Copper is something that gets dyed, then becomes able to dye.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:46-63`
  - Zosimos says the four bodies both dye and are dyed; Maria is cited inside this discussion.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:70-80`
  - Weight loss, whitening, yellowing, sulfurous matter, and boiling are all tied to the same technical sequence.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:232-246`
  - The art is said to speak `περὶ μιᾶς βαφῆς`; Maria works through the plate of the kerotakis; after blackening and whitening comes secure yellowing.
- `workbench/sources/alchem.md:268-281`
  - Maria says copper is dyed first and then dyes; `ὁ χαλκὸς οὐ βάπτει, ἀλλὰ βάπτεται`.
- `workbench/sources/Alchemy.md:45-50`
  - Pliny's perfume note that color is a real component of perfume can be used only as light support for the broader shared logic of managed color.

Allowed claims:
- Dye-language is central, not incidental, in these passages.
- Copper is treated as a substrate that undergoes staged color/process change.
- The overlap with perfumery is strongest at the level of handled materials and color-treatment logic.

Do not infer:
- That metallic `βαφή` is identical to textile dyeing in every respect.
- That Pliny's perfume color note proves an alchemical theory.
- That the post can claim modern “chemistry” equivalence.

Overlap to avoid:
- `perfume-colors`
- `witchs-art`

Drafting note:
- The hook should be blunt: copper gets dyed before it dyes anything else.
