## `frankincense-snakes`

Series: `MATERIA`

Conceptual spine:
Herodotus says frankincense was gathered by smoking winged snakes away from the trees with styrax.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:19`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:22`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:27`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:31-33`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:45`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:51`

Allowed claims:
- Arabia alone produces frankincense, myrrh, cassia, cinnamon, and labdanum in this passage.
- Herodotus says all of these except myrrh are difficult for Arabs to obtain.
- Frankincense is collected by burning styrax.
- The frankincense trees are guarded by many small winged snakes of varied appearance.
- Styrax smoke is the one thing that drives them away.
- Herodotus expands the story by explaining why deadly creatures do not overrun the earth and says the winged snakes are concentrated in Arabia alone.

Do not infer:
- That Herodotus is giving a botanical account we should flatten into literal natural history.
- Pliny's taxes, caravan routes, sacred harvesters, or Roman prices.
- General serpent symbolism beyond this collection story.

Overlap to avoid:
- `tus-supply-chain`
- `fire-test`

## `cassia-cinnamon-lore`

Series: `MATERIA`

Conceptual spine:
In Herodotus, cassia and cinnamon each come with their own animal obstacle and harvesting trick.

Key passages:
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:19`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:56`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:61`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:64`
- `workbench/sources/herod3.106-112:68-70`

Allowed claims:
- Cassia grows in or around a shallow lake.
- Fierce winged creatures, especially bat-like in appearance, stay around the cassia.
- Gatherers wrap their whole body and face in hides or skins, leaving only the eyes, before cutting cassia.
- Herodotus says cinnamon's origin is not clearly known.
- Large birds carry what Greeks call cinnamon to mud nests on inaccessible cliffs.
- Arabs bring large cut limbs of cattle, donkeys, and other pack animals; the birds carry the meat to the nests, which then break under the weight, and collectors gather the fallen cinnamon.

Do not infer:
- Pliny's debunking frame or later supply-chain replacement.
- That Herodotus is describing a single collection method for all Arabian aromatics.
- Any labdanum/goat material from chapter 112.

Overlap to avoid:
- `cinnamon-fables`
- `labdanum-goat`
