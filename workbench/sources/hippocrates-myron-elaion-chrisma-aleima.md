# Hippocrates on `myron`, `elaion`, `chrisma`, `aleima`

Curated run through [Hippocrates](/home/seancoughlin/Projects/social/workbench/sources/Hippocrates), tracking lemma plus derivatives for four families:

- `μύρον`
- `ἔλαιον`
- `χρῖσμα / χρίειν / διαχρίειν / ἐγχρίειν`
- `ἄλειμμα / ἀλείφειν / ἐπαλείφειν`

This is not a full concordance. It is an editorial source map for the passages that are most useful for drafting and for understanding how Hippocratic texts actually use these terms.

## Editorial takeaways

1. The center of gravity is gynecological.
   The overwhelming bulk of relevant material sits in `De natura muliebri` and `De mulierum affectibus i–iii`.

2. `μύρον` is usually a perfumed, named preparation.
   In practice it is often `ῥόδινον`, `Αἰγύπτιον λευκόν`, `ναρκίσσινον`, `ἰρῖνον`, or another scented preparation used in fumigation, pessaries, or topical treatment.

3. `ἔλαιον` is broader and noisier.
   It often functions as carrier, cooking/boiling medium, bath additive, or plain oil. Many occurrences are technically relevant but not editorially strong.

4. `χρῖσμα` matters mostly as an action family, not as a noun.
   The important forms in this file are verbs and derivatives like `διαχρίειν`, `ἐγχρίειν`, `περιχρίειν`, and the abstract plurals `χρίσμασιν`, `ἐγχρίσμασιν`.

5. `ἄλειμμα` is a preparation word.
   It often names the ointment/unguent itself, especially in softening and uterine treatment recipes.

6. Repetition is heavy.
   The omnibus file contains repeated recipe clusters. This run keeps the strongest representative version rather than counting each near-duplicate separately.

## High-value passages

### 1. `μύρον` as scented intervention in a case history

- Work: `De morbis popularibus (= Epidemiae)`
- Reference: `Book 4, chapter 1, section 30, line 8`
- Family: `μύρον`
- Bucket: diagnostic or sensory smell
- Keep because: this is the cleanest non-gynecological case where a scented application is clinically consequential.

> Προσθεμένη εὐῶδες ἐξ ἀλήτου καὶ μύρου, ξυνέβη ταχέως ἀναδοθῆναι καὶ τελευτῆσαι.

The perfume is not ornamental. It is part of a decisive clinical event.

### 2. `διαχρίειν` and `μύρον` in uterine directionality therapy

- Work: `De locis in homine`
- Reference: `Section 47, line 27`
- Family: `χρῖσμα` plus `μύρον`
- Bucket: topical application or anointing; uterine therapy
- Keep because: this is the clearest early passage where the verbal family carries the treatment logic.

> ἢν μὲν προέλθῃ μοῦνον καὶ ᾖ διαχρίειν, χρῶ ᾧ τινι βούλει τῶν κακόδμων...

> ἢν δὲ ἀνακεχωρήκῃ ... τοῖσιν εὐόδμοισι προσθετοῖσι χρῶ ... σμύρνῃ, ἢ μύρῳ...

The opposition is structural: foul-smelling applications for one uterine state, fragrant ones for another.

### 3. `μύρον ῥόδινον` in fumigation and intercourse sequence

- Work: `De natura muliebri`
- Reference: `Section 16, line 14`
- Family: `μύρον`
- Bucket: fumigation or uterine therapy
- Keep because: it shows rose perfume embedded in a procedural sequence, not as a luxury extra.

> ὑποθυμιήσθω τοῖσιν ἀρώμασι, νέτωπον περιχέασα καὶ μύρον ῥόδινον· ἔπειτα τῷ ἀνδρὶ ξυνίτω.

### 4. White olives before oil, then rose perfume as corrective

- Work: `De natura muliebri`
- Reference: `Section 32, line 29`
- Family: `ἔλαιον` plus `μύρον`
- Bucket: carrier/base medium; topical correction
- Keep because: this one passage gives two distinct uses, one around the making of oil and one around dipping a pessary in rose perfume.

> ἐλαίας λευκὰς πρόσθεν ἢ ἔλαιον εἶναι κατακνήσας ξήρανον...

> ὁκόταν δὲ δάκνῃ, ἀφαιρευμένη, ἐς ῥόδινον μύρον ἐμβάπτουσα, προστιθέσθω...

### 5. Dense fumigation dossier with oil and perfume side by side

- Work: `De natura muliebri`
- Reference: `Section 34, line 6`
- Family: `ἔλαιον` plus `μύρον`
- Bucket: fumigation or uterine therapy
- Keep because: this is the strongest recipe cluster for showing how oil and perfume function as wetting agents for aromatics.

Selected phrases:

> ἐλαίης φύλλοις ... φυρήσας ἐλαίῳ, ὑποθυμιῇν.

> Σκαμμωνίην, σμύρναν, λιβανωτὸν, μύρον περιχέας, ὑποθυμιῇν.

> Θεῖον ἐλαίῳ φώκης ἀναδεύσας, ὑποθυμιῇν.

> μύρον ῥόδινον δεύσας, θυμιῇν.

This is one of the best passages for the practical difference between dry aromatics and aromatics activated by oil/perfume media.

### 6. `ἄλειφα` and white Egyptian unguent in a softening recipe

- Work: `De natura muliebri`
- Reference: `Section 109, line 75`
- Family: `μύρον`, `ἔλαιον`, `ἄλειμμα`
- Bucket: topical application or anointing; softening preparation
- Keep because: this is the clearest gynecological passage where `ἄλειφα` is doing real lexical work.

> μύρον ναρκίσσινον ... ῥόδινον ἄλειφα...

> καταδῆσαι καὶ ἐμβάψαι ἐς ἄλειφα λευκὸν Αἰγύπτιον...

This is a core passage for treating `ἄλειμμα` as a preparation class rather than just an act of smearing.

### 7. `μύρῳ` first, then `ἐλαίῳ`: clear contrast of media

- Work: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii`
- Reference: `Section 19, line 3`
- Family: `μύρον` plus `ἔλαιον`
- Bucket: carrier/base medium
- Keep because: it cleanly stages two different media in sequence.

> στυπτηρίην λείην τρίψας, διεὶς μύρῳ ... προστίθει...

> τῇ δὲ τετάρτῃ, χολὴν βοὸς αὔην ἐν ἐλαίῳ ἀναζέσας ... πρόσθες...

### 8. The densest mixed passage for all four families

- Work: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii`
- Reference: `Section 74, line 24`
- Family: `μύρον`, `ἔλαιον`, `ἄλειμμα`, `χρῖσμα`
- Bucket: topical application or anointing; uterine therapy
- Keep because: this is the single richest passage in the file for the cluster as a whole.

Selected phrases:

> χηνὸς ἔλαιον καὶ νέτωπον καὶ ῥητίνην...

> πρόσθες ἐν εἰρίῳ καὶ ῥοδίνῳ μύρῳ.

> μύρον ῥόδινον καὶ γάλα γυναικὸς ... ἐναλείφειν τὸ στόμα τῆς μήτρης.

> ταῦτα τήξας ἐν μύρῳ ῥοδίνῳ ... ποίησον ὡς κηρωτήν· εἶτα τούτῳ χλιερῷ ἐναλείφειν...

> μέλιτι καὶ ἐλαίῳ χηνὸς μίξας, προστίθει.

If one passage is needed to show how the terms overlap without collapsing into synonyms, use this one.

### 9. Perfumed topical treatment for hair loss

- Work: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii`
- Reference: `Section 189, line 2`
- Family: `μύρον` plus `χρῖσμα`
- Bucket: topical application or anointing
- Keep because: it is not uterine, and it shows perfumed topical medicine on the body surface.

> λήδανον μετὰ ῥοδίνου ἢ ἀνθινοῦ μύρου τρῖβε, καὶ μετ' οἴνου ἐγχριέτω...

### 10. A full Hippocratic perfume-making logic inside therapy

- Work: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii`
- Reference: `Section 206, line 11`
- Family: all four families are represented across the block
- Bucket: preparation/manufacture language; uterine therapy
- Keep because: the text explicitly refers to ingredients "thrown into perfumes" and coordinates `ἀλείφα`, `ἔλαιον`, `μύρον`, and `περιχρίειν`.

Selected phrases:

> ῥοδίνου ἀλείφατος ἡμικοτύλην...

> ἅπαντα ὁκόσα ἐς τὰ μύρα ἐμβάλλεται, κόψαι καὶ κατασῆσαι...

> περιχρίειν νετώπῳ, ῥόδινον ἔλαιον ὡς ἄριστον, καὶ αἰγύπτιον λευκὸν...

> ὀργάσασθαι αἰγυπτίῳ λευκῷ μύρῳ...

This is one of the best passages for showing that the Hippocratic corpus thinks in pharmaceutical forms, not just pleasant smells.

### 11. `χρίσματι` and `ἐγχρίειν` in wound treatment

- Work: `De morbis i–iii`
- Reference: `Book 2, section 36, line 5`
- Family: `χρῖσμα`
- Bucket: topical application or anointing
- Keep because: this is the cleanest non-gynecological medical use of the family.

> ἰῆσθαι τὸ ἕλκος τῷ χρίσματι ἐναλείφων...

> καὶ ἐπὴν περισαπῇ, ἐγχρίειν τὸ ἄνθος τὸ ἐν τῷ μέλιτι...

### 12. Abstract therapeutic categories: `κατάχρισμα`, `ἄλειμμα`

- Work: `De alimento`
- Reference: `Section 16, line 2`
- Family: `χρῖσμα` plus `ἄλειμμα`
- Bucket: abstract medical classification
- Keep because: this is the strongest noun-level evidence for the family outside recipes.

> Ἐς δὲ ταύτην, ἔξωθεν μὲν, κατάπλασμα, κατάχρισμα, ἄλειμμα...

### 13. Abstract plurals: `χρίσμασιν`, `ἐγχρίσμασιν`

- Work: `De humoribus`
- Reference: `Section 5, line 15`
- Family: `χρῖσμα`
- Bucket: abstract medical classification
- Keep because: it confirms that anointing applications belong to the recognized menu of therapeutic manipulations.

> ... ὑγρῆναι, ξηρῆναι, χρίσμασιν, ἐγχρίσμασιν, ἐπιπλάστοισιν...

## Term-family notes

### `μύρον`

- Best sense in this file: perfumed medicinal preparation.
- Strongest forms: `μύρον ῥόδινον`, `Αἰγύπτιον λευκόν`, `ναρκίσσινον`, `ἀνθινόν`, `ἰρῖνον`.
- Strongest functions: fumigation, pessary wetting, topical softening, named aromatic vehicle.

### `ἔλαιον`

- Best sense in this file: oil as a broader medium, often less marked than `μύρον`.
- Strongest functions: carrier, boiling medium, bath additive, wetting agent for fumigations, plain oil in topical treatment.
- Editorial caution: many `ἔλαιον` hits in `De diaeta`, `De affectionibus interioribus`, and similar texts are too general to carry much perfume-specific weight.

### `χρῖσμα / χρίειν`

- Best sense in this file: the act or category of anointing/application.
- Strongest forms here: `διαχρίειν`, `ἐγχρίειν`, `περιχρίειν`, `χρίσματι`, `χρίσμασιν`, `ἐγχρίσμασιν`.
- Editorial caution: if the goal is perfume or unguent history, the verb family often matters more than the noun.

### `ἄλειμμα / ἀλείφειν`

- Best sense in this file: ointment/unguent as a preparation or a softened application medium.
- Strongest forms here: `ῥόδινον ἄλειφα`, `ἄλειφα λευκὸν Αἰγύπτιον`, `ἀλείφατος`, `ἐναλείφειν`.
- Strongest functions: softening the uterine mouth, coating/wetting a prepared pessary, topical smearing.

## What to deprioritize

- Plain dietetic or gymnastic `ἔλαιον` in `De diaeta i–iv`.
- Generic wound/ulcer oil where the aromatic or lexical distinction is weak.
- Repeated gynecological recipe clusters that do not add a new term-function pairing.

## Appendix: Named perfumes and oils

This appendix tracks the named preparations that are explicitly labeled as perfumes, oils, or unguents in the Hippocrates file. It does not try to list every aromatic ingredient that can be thrown into an unnamed compound. Translations are mine. Where the Greek name is uncertain in English, I transliterate it and note the uncertainty.

### Perfumery-style names

- `ῥόδινον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 16.14; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206.11.
  Greek: `μύρον ῥόδινον`; `ῥόδινον ἔλαιον`; `ῥόδινον ἄλειφα`.
  Translation: `rose perfume`; `rose oil`; `rose unguent`.
  Note: this is the commonest named preparation in the file.

- `αἰγύπτιον`, especially `λευκὸν αἰγύπτιον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 109.75; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206.11; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162.29.
  Greek: `ἄλειφα λευκὸν Αἰγύπτιον`; `αἰγυπτίῳ λευκῷ μύρῳ`; `ἔλαιον λευκὸν αἰγύπτιον`.
  Translation: `White Egyptian unguent`; `White Egyptian perfume`; `White Egyptian oil`.
  Note: the file treats `Egyptian` and `White Egyptian` as strong product labels, not just provenance tags.

- `νέτωπον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 16.14; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206.11; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162.29.
  Greek: `νέτωπον περιχέασα`; `περιχρίειν νετώπῳ`; `ἐμβάπτειν ἐς νέτωπον`.
  Translation: `after pouring on netopon`; `to anoint with netopon`; `to dip it into netopon`.
  Note: I keep this as `netopon/netopion` rather than forcing a modern gloss. In context it behaves like a named aromatic preparation.

- `ναρκίσσινον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 109.75; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162.29.
  Greek: `μύρον ναρκίσσινον`; `ἔλαιον ναρκίσσινον`.
  Translation: `narcissus perfume`; `narcissus oil`.

- `ἴρινον / ἰρίνῳ`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74.24; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 133.138; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162.29.
  Greek: `μύρῳ ἰρίνῳ`; `ἴρινον`; `ἰρίνῳ`.
  Translation: `iris perfume`; `iris oil`.

- `ἀνθινόν / ἀνθίνῳ`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 189.2; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162.29.
  Greek: `ῥοδίνου ἢ ἀνθινοῦ μύρου`; `ἀνθίνῳ`.
  Translation: `flower perfume`; `flower oil`.
  Note: this is a generic floral label, not a single botanical species.

- `μυρσῖνον`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126.15; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206.11.
  Greek: `ἢ μυρσίνῳ`; `ἐς δὲ τὼ ὦτε μύρσινον`.
  Translation: `myrtle perfume/oil`.

- `μήλινον`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206.11.
  Greek: `μύρσινον ἢ μήλινον`.
  Translation: `mēlinon, probably apple- or quince-scented oil/perfume`.
  Note: the exact plant behind `μήλινον` is not certain enough here to force a single English equivalent.

- `βακχάριον`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126.15.
  Greek: `ἢ λευκῷ αἰγυπτίῳ ἢ μυρσίνῳ ἢ βακχαρίῳ ἢ ἀμαρακίνῳ`.
  Translation: `or in White Egyptian, or myrtle, or bakkharion, or amarakinon`.
  Note: I transliterate `βακχάριον`; the file clearly treats it as one named aromatic option among standard unguents.

- `ἀμαράκινον`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126.15; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 133.138.
  Greek: `ἢ ἀμαρακίνῳ`; `μύρον αἰγύπτιον ὡς βέλτιστον, ἢ ἀμαράκινον`.
  Translation: `amarakinon`, that is, sweet-marjoram perfume.

- `ὀποβαλσάμιον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 34.6.
  Greek: `μύρῳ ὀποβαλσάμῳ δεύσας`.
  Translation: `after wetting it with balsam perfume` or `with opobalsamum perfume`.

- `σούσινον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 86.2; `De natura muliebri` 74.1; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 196.3.
  Greek: `ἔλαιον καὶ σούσινον μίξας`; `σούσινον καὶ βάτου γλῶσσαν`; `σούσινον ... ἐν εἰρίῳ ποιεῖν προσθετόν`.
  Translation: `mix oil and sousinon`; `apply sousinon with bramble-tongue`; `make a pessary of sousinon`.
  Note: this is likely the usual `sousinon`, the lily preparation. One odd line in `De natura muliebri` 32.137 reads `Σούσινον [ὅ ἐστι γῆ Αἰγυπτίη]`, which looks like a different gloss and should not be taken as straightforward perfume evidence.

- `μύρον μαλθακόν`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 203.33.
  Greek: `μύρῳ μαλθακῷ`.
  Translation: `soft perfume` or `mild/softened perfume`.
  Note: this looks more like a descriptive product-type than a fixed botanical name, but it is still a labeled perfume in the file.

### Other explicitly named oils that recur

- `κέδρινον ἔλαιον`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78.196.
  Greek: `βούτυρον καὶ κέδρινον ἔλαιον ἐν μέλιτι ὀλίγῳ`.
  Translation: `butter and cedar oil with a little honey`.

- `ἔλαιον φώκης`
  Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126.15; `De natura muliebri` 34.6.
  Greek: `τὰς δὲ ῥῖνας διαλεῖψαι ... ἔλαιον φώκης`; `θεῖον ἐλαίῳ φώκης ἀναδεύσας`.
  Translation: `anoint the nostrils with seal oil`; `mix sulfur with seal oil`.
  Note: not a perfume, but a recurring named oil with a distinct medical role.

- `χηνὸς ἔλαιον / χήνειον ἔλαιον`
  Refs: `De natura muliebri` 74.1; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74.24.
  Greek: `χηνὸς ἔλαιον καὶ νέτωπον καὶ ῥητίνην`; `μέλιτι καὶ ἐλαίῳ χηνὸς μίξας`.
  Translation: `goose oil with netopon and resin`; `mixed with honey and goose oil`.
  Note: again, not a perfume name, but too frequent and explicit to ignore in a named-oils appendix.

### Named perfumery-style preparations and bare brands

#### `ῥόδινον`

Refs: `De natura muliebri` 6, 8, 16, 32, 34, 97, 109; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 37, 74, 77, 78, 84, 133, 135, 136, 158, 162, 195, 202, 205, 206, 209; `De superfetatione` 33, 39.

- `De natura muliebri` 6: `σμύρναν ῥόδινον περιχέων` = `pouring rose perfume over myrrh`; rose used as the perfumed vehicle in a uterine remedy.
- `De natura muliebri` 8: `τῇ δ' ὑστεραίῃ ῥόδινον μύρον` = `on the following day, rose perfume`; a follow-up application after `νέτωπον`.
- `De natura muliebri` 16: `νέτωπον περιχέασα καὶ μύρον ῥόδινον` = `after pouring on netopon and rose perfume`; the pair appears as a seductive or stimulating aromatic sequence.
- `De natura muliebri` 32: `ἐς ῥόδινον μύρον ἐμβά-`; `μύρον ῥόδινον` = `dip it into rose perfume`; `rose perfume`; dipped wool/pessary application and a separate rose-perfume rag.
- `De natura muliebri` 34: `μύρον ῥόδινον δεύσας, θυμιῇν` = `wet it with rose perfume and fumigate`; rose perfume serves as a fumigation medium.
- `De natura muliebri` 97: `ῥόδινον μύρον καὶ κηρὸν` = `rose perfume and wax`; perfumed pessary mixture.
- `De natura muliebri` 109: `ῥόδινον ἄλειφα` = `rose unguent`; listed among strong topical aromatic options.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 37: `κἄπειτα νέτωπον καὶ ῥόδινον ἔλαιον ὡς εὐωδέστατον` = `and then netopon and rose oil, as fragrant as possible`; explicit recommendation for the best-smelling rose oil.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74: `μύρον ῥόδινον καὶ γάλα γυναικὸς`; `ἐς ἄλειφα ῥόδινον ἢ αἰγύπτιον` = `rose perfume and woman's milk`; `into rose or Egyptian unguent`; rose appears both as perfume and as a dipping medium.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 77: `δαφνίδας καὶ ῥόδινον ἔλαιον` = `laurel leaves and rose oil`; rose oil in an obstetric aromatic bundle.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78: `ῥόδινον εἴριον ἀποβάπτουσα`; `μύρον τε ῥόδινον ἐπιχέας` = `dipping wool into rose`; `pouring in rose perfume`; both pessary and klysma contexts.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 84: `ῥόδινον ἄλειφα`; `προστιθέσθω ῥόδινον` = `rose unguent`; `let rose be applied`; topical option in repeated recipe clusters.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 133: `ἐς ἔλαιον ῥόδινον ἢ ἴρινον` = `into rose oil or iris oil`; rose offered as one of the preferred oils in a procedural setting.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 135: `τῇ δὲ ὑστεραίῃ ἔλαιον ῥόδινον` = `on the next day, rose oil`; next-day follow-up.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 136: `... λευκὸν ἢ ῥόδινον ...` = `... White Egyptian or rose ...`; rose stands as the alternative to White Egyptian.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 158: `ἔλαιον ῥόδινον`; `μύρον ῥόδινον` = `rose oil`; `rose perfume`; softening/anointing recipe family.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162: `τῇ δ' ὑστεραίῃ ἔλαιον ῥόδινον ἐν εἰρίῳ` = `on the next day, rose oil in wool`; wool dipped in rose oil.

- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 202: `ἐπιχέαι ῥόδινον ἔλαιον` = `pour on rose oil`; rose oil poured over fragrant wine.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 205: `καὶ λουσαμένη ἔλαιον ῥόδινον`; `ῥόδινον μύρον` = `after bathing, rose oil`; `rose perfume`; both oil and perfume forms appear.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206: `ῥόδινον ἔλαιον ὡς ἄριστον` = `rose oil, as good as possible`; strongest quality language in the Hippocratic rose file.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 209: `μῖξαι δὲ ἔλαιον χηνὸς καὶ ῥόδινον` = `mix goose oil and rose`; rose used as the scented component in a klysma.
- `De superfetatione` 33: `ῥόδινον` = `rose`; perfumed ingredient in a uterine softening compound.
- `De superfetatione` 39: `νέτωπον, ῥόδινον μύρον, χηνὸς ἄλειφαρ` = `netopon, rose perfume, goose unguent`; rose perfume in a softening pessary.

#### `αἰγύπτιον / λευκὸν αἰγύπτιον`

Refs: `De natura muliebri` 7, 32, 33, 34; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126, 162, 206; `De superfetatione` 33.

- `De natura muliebri` 7: `τῷ Αἰγυπτίῳ μύρῳ τὰς ὑστέρας` = `with Egyptian perfume to the womb`; a diagnostic/perceptual use keyed to whether the smell reaches the nose.
- `De natura muliebri` 32: `μύρῳ ῥοδίνῳ καὶ Αἰγυπτίῳ`; `μύρῳ Αἰγυπτίῳ λευκῷ ἢ ῥοδίνῳ` = `with rose and Egyptian perfume`; `with White Egyptian perfume or rose`; White Egyptian is clearly a product label here.
- `De natura muliebri` 33: `τὸ Αἰγύπτιον` = `the Egyptian`; a bare brand-name use inside a mixed uterine recipe.
- `De natura muliebri` 34: `μύρῳ Αἰγυπτίῳ λευκῷ θυμιῇν` = `fumigate with White Egyptian perfume`; one of the strongest perfume-brand passages.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126: `βάψας ἢ λευκῷ αἰγυπτίῳ ἢ μυρσίνῳ ἢ βακχαρίῳ ἢ ἀμαρακίνῳ` = `dip it in White Egyptian, or myrsinon, or bakkharion, or amarakinon`; a clean bare-brand option list.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162: `ἐμβαψάτω ἐν λευκῷ ἐλαίῳ αἰγυπτίῳ` = `let her dip it in White Egyptian oil`; explicit oil form.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206: `αἰγυπτίῳ λευκῷ μύρῳ καὶ νετώπῳ` = `with White Egyptian perfume and netopon`; White Egyptian paired with another named aromatic.
- `De superfetatione` 33: `λευκῷ αἰγυπτίῳ εὐώδει` = `fragrant White Egyptian`; explicit emphasis on scent quality.

#### `νέτωπον`

- `Epidemiae` 5.1.66: `ἐγχεῖν ἔλαιον ἢ νέτωπον` = `pour in oil or netopon`; netopon offered as a standard aromatic alternative to oil.
- `Epidemiae` 7.1.63: `ἐγχέειν ἔλαιον ἢ νέτωπον` = `pour in oil or netopon`; same practical pairing.
- `De natura muliebri` 8: `νέτωπον προσθέσθαι` = `apply netopon`; followed on the next day by rose perfume.
- `De natura muliebri` 16: `νέτωπον περιχέασα` = `after pouring on netopon`; explicit fragrant application.
- `De natura muliebri` 32: `νέτωπον ξυμμίξας`; `ἐς νέτωπον` = `mixing in netopon`; `into netopon`; used as a dip or incorporated aromatic.
- `De natura muliebri` 33: `νέτωπον, ῥητίνην, μέλι ... τὸ Αἰγύπτιον` = `netopon, resin, honey ... the Egyptian`; bare brand-name sequence in a compound.
- `De natura muliebri` 74: `χηνὸς ἔλαιον καὶ νέτωπον καὶ ῥητίνην` = `goose oil and netopon and resin`; one of the clearest mixed-medium passages.
- `De natura muliebri` 97: `νέτωπον καὶ μίσυ ἐν ῥοδίνῳ μύρῳ` = `netopon and misy in rose perfume`; netopon inside a strongly perfumed matrix.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 37: `κἄπειτα νέτωπον καὶ ῥόδινον ἔλαιον` = `and then netopon and rose oil`; netopon paired with premium rose oil.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74: `χηνὸς ἔλαιον καὶ νέτωπον καὶ ῥητίνην` = `goose oil and netopon and resin`; standard uterine application cluster.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78: `χαλβάνην καὶ νέτωπον`; `νέτωπον ἴσον ἑκάστου` = `galbanum and netopon`; `an equal amount of netopon`; both pessary and klysma settings.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 84: `νέτωπον, κυκλαμίνου` = `netopon, cyclamen`; netopon in a strong topical formula.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 135: `τὸ νέτωπον` = `the netopon`; bare-brand follow-up instruction.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 157: `τῇ δὲ ὑστεραίῃ τὸ νέτωπον` = `on the next day, the netopon`; netopon follows narcissus.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 158: `νέτωπον, ἔλαιον ῥόδινον` = `netopon, rose oil`; mixed softening recipe.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162: `ἐμβάπτειν ἐς νέτωπον` = `dip into netopon`; one of the clearest uses of netopon as dipping medium.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 200: `σμύρνην, ἢ ῥητίνην, ἢ νέτωπον` = `myrrh, or resin, or netopon`; netopon listed among aromatic choices.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 205: `νέτωπον, χολὴν ταύρου, νίτρον, κυκλάμινον` = `netopon, bull's bile, nitre, cyclamen`; strong compound with netopon at the front.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 206: `καὶ νέτωπον`; `περιχρίειν νετώπῳ` = `and netopon`; `to anoint with netopon`; probably the strongest explicit verbal pairing.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 209: `χυλὸν μυρσίνης, λιβανωτὸν, νέτωπον ἴσον` = `myrtle juice, frankincense, an equal amount of netopon`; netopon in a klysma blend.
- `De superfetatione` 33: `ῥητίνη τερεβινθίνη ἢ νέτωπον`; `κιννάμωμον, νέτωπον` = `turpentine resin or netopon`; `cinnamon, netopon`; netopon appears as a named aromatic choice.
- `De superfetatione` 39: `νέτωπον, ῥόδινον μύρον, χηνὸς ἄλειφαρ` = `netopon, rose perfume, goose unguent`; another clean brand sequence.

#### `ναρκίσσινον`

- `De natura muliebri` 8: `ξὺν τῷ ναρκισσίνῳ` = `with the narcissus preparation`; narcissus is used first, then netopon.

- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 66: `ἐλαίῳ ναρκισσίνῳ` = `with narcissus oil`; topical soothing/anointing context.

#### `ἴρινον / ἰρίνῳ`

Refs: `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74, 78, 133, 158, 161, 162, 206, 208, 235.

- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 74: `μύρῳ ἰρίνῳ`; `μύρον ἴρινον` = `with iris perfume`; `iris perfume`; both application and named-product list.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78: `ἴρινον μύρον` = `iris perfume`; the preparation is used in a soft pessary recipe.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 133: `ἐς ἔλαιον ... ἴρινον`; `ἴρινον ἄκρον` = `into iris oil`; `the best iris perfume`; high-value quality marker.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 158: `ῥοδίνῳ ἢ ἰρίνῳ ἐλαίῳ` = `with rose or iris oil`; scented oil options in a softening mixture.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 161: `ἴρινον μύρον ξὺν μέλιτι` = `iris perfume with honey`; direct perfume use.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 162: `ἀνθίνῳ, ἢ ἰρίνῳ ὡς καλλίστῳ` = `with flower oil or iris oil, whichever is best`; strongest explicit preference language for iris oil.

- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 208: `ἐν χηνὸς στέατι καὶ μύρῳ ἰρίνῳ` = `in goose fat and iris perfume`; iris perfume as the fragrant medium.

### Other explicitly named oils

#### `κέδρινον ἔλαιον`

Refs: `De natura muliebri` 33; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78, 90, 91; `De ulceribus` 21.

- `De natura muliebri` 33: `βούτυρον, κέδρινον ἔλαιον` = `butter, cedar oil`; cedar oil already appears in the same klysma family that later recurs in `De mulierum affectibus`.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 78: `βούτυρον καὶ κέδρινον ἔλαιον`; `κέδρινον ἔλαιον παραχέας ὀλίγον` = `butter and cedar oil`; `pouring on a little cedar oil`; two closely related klysma variants.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 90: `κέδρινον ἔλαιον, μέλι παραμίσγειν` = `mix in cedar oil and honey`; explicit klysma medium.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 91: `κέδρινον ἔλαιον ... δὸς πιεῖν` = `cedar oil ... give it to drink`; medicinal internal use.
- `De ulceribus` 21: `κέδρινον` = `cedar-oil component`; cedar appears as part of ulcer treatment, though not in a perfumery frame.

#### `ἔλαιον φώκης`

Refs: `De natura muliebri` 34; `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126, 133, 203; `De exsectione foetus` 5.

- `De natura muliebri` 34: `θεῖον ἐλαίῳ φώκης ἀναδεύσας`; `ἐλαίῳ φώκης δεύσας` = `mix sulfur with seal oil`; `wetting it with seal oil`; repeated fumigation medium.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 126: `ῥῖνας διαλεῖψαι ... ἔλαιον φώκης`; `θυμιῇν φώκης ἔλαιον` = `anoint the nostrils with seal oil`; `fumigate with seal oil`; the clearest non-perfume therapeutic use.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 133: `φώκης ἔλαιον` = `seal oil`; used for warming/poultice treatment.
- `De mulierum affectibus i–iii` 203: `ἐλαίῳ φώκης δεύσας`; `τῷ ἐλαίῳ τῆς φώκης` = `wetting with seal oil`; `with the oil of the seal`; recurrent fumigation cluster.
- `De exsectione foetus` 5: `ἀλείψας φώκης ἐλαίῳ` = `having anointed with seal oil`; procedural surgical/obstetric use.
