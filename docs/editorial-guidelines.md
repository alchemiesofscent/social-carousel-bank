# Editorial Guidelines

Derived on 2026-03-24 from:

- `report.md`
- `workbench/report.md`
- the WP6 published-revision pilot in `workbench/revisions/`

This document defines the working voice of the project and the minimal-change editorial standard to use before a human copy editor reviews published or draft carousel text.

## Purpose

The project already has a strong premise engine. The main problem is not weak ideas. The main problem is that body slides often carry more scholarship than a phone screen can comfortably hold.

The editing goal is therefore narrow:

- keep the voice
- preserve strong hooks
- reduce reader friction
- cut catalog mode
- make closers land harder

Do not rewrite for polish alone. Revise only where the change clearly improves readability, flow, or audience fit.

## Core Voice

The house voice is:

- discovery-led
- materially grounded
- academically serious
- curious rather than hectoring
- concise enough for mobile reading

It should feel like a scholar noticing something strange in the source and showing why it matters.

It should not feel like:

- lecture notes
- thread-style hot takes
- brand copy
- winky internet history writing
- faux-poetic vagueness

## Audience

Write for an Instagram reader who is intelligent, curious, and willing to follow scholarship, but who is not already inside the philology or ancient-medicine discourse.

Assume the reader can handle:

- one unfamiliar proper noun
- one foreign-language term
- one conceptual turn per slide

Do not assume the reader can comfortably process all three at once.

## Non-Negotiable Principles

1. One slide, one job.
2. The body must cash out the hook, not merely surround it.
3. A list needs a payoff: image, consequence, argument, or social meaning.
4. Concrete anchors beat abstract explanation.
5. One vivid example usually beats three compressed examples.
6. Closers should reframe or sharpen, not merely summarize.
7. Preserve uncertainty honestly, but do not make uncertainty the whole post.
8. If a post is already strong, do less.

## Hook Rules

Good hooks in this project usually do one of four things:

- present a strange concrete fact
- set up a sharp contrast
- make a category error visible
- frame a single artifact or word as evidence of a larger world

Hook guidelines:

- Keep the claim slightly tighter than you think you need.
- Do not overdramatize if the body is more measured.
- The subtext should deepen the hook, not repeat it.
- If the hook promises process, the first body slide must reach process quickly.
- If the hook promises a social claim, the body must not dissolve into raw source inventory.

## Body Rules

The body is where most posts weaken.

Preferred body movement:

1. orient the reader in plain language
2. give the evidence
3. cash it out into consequence or meaning

Body guidelines:

- Cover-test every body slide. If covering the slide does not break the argument, merge or cut it.
- Do not stack transliteration, quotation, translation, and interpretation in one opening sentence unless the quote itself is the point.
- Break additive example chains after the second example unless accumulation is itself the argument.
- If a recipe post is really about process, move process earlier than naming lore or etymology.
- If a marketplace post is really about social shape, move from one example to one escalation, not to a full catalog.
- Keep the strongest image-bearing sentence visible. Do not bury it under setup.

## Accessibility Rules

Main friction points observed in the bank:

- proper-name stacking
- multiple technical terms on one slide
- long quotations
- unit and ingredient overload
- overflow fixes that split one weak idea into two weak slides

Accessibility guidelines:

- Introduce one unfamiliar name at a time.
- Use Greek, Latin, and Egyptian strategically, not decoratively.
- Translate quickly.
- Paraphrase before quoting when the quotation is dense.
- If the quantities are the point, keep them. If not, compress them.
- Prefer short declarative sentences over clause chains.
- A slide should be legible on first pass without rereading.

## Closer Rules

A closer should feel earned, not pasted on.

Strong closers in this project usually:

- turn scale into image
- reveal the social meaning of a detail
- compress the argument into one memorable consequence
- reframe the post through material, bodily, or institutional stakes

Avoid closers that:

- simply restate the hook
- broaden into a generic moral
- lean on a modern slogan
- add a fresh claim too late

## Tone Controls

Preferred diction:

- direct
- exact
- concrete
- lightly surprising

Avoid:

- internet-coded phrasing
- modern marketing metaphors unless the post is intentionally plain and contemporary
- smug skepticism
- too much rhetorical wink
- “writer-forward” lines that call attention to the narration rather than the material

Replace:

- punchline language with a sharper fact
- contemporary analogy with a source-shaped image
- generalized “this tells us” endings with a more precise consequence

## Revision Depth Ladder

Use the lightest effective intervention.

### `pass-through`

Use when:

- the hook, body flow, and closer already work
- changes would mostly be cosmetic
- machine editing would risk flattening the voice

### `surgical`

Use when the problem is:

- repeated phrasing
- one overloaded slide
- mild term-load
- a weaker closer
- slight tonal drift

Preferred moves:

- cut one sentence
- simplify one sentence
- move one phrase from quote-heavy to plain language
- tighten subtext
- sharpen closer

### `surgical-structural`

Use only when reports clearly show:

- a slide fails the cover test
- the post is stuck in catalog mode
- sequencing is the real problem

Allowed moves:

- merge one body slide into a stronger neighbor
- cut one weak body slide
- move one body slide earlier

Not allowed:

- hook replacement without cause
- full rewrite from scratch
- expanding the conceptual scope

## Agent Workflow

The working loop is:

1. PM prepares the batch.
2. Writer revises from the published or assigned draft base.
3. Editor reviews the rewrite against the rubric and the revision class.
4. PM decides:
   - `pass`
   - `re-revise`
   - `leave for human`

Max two machine rounds per batch.

## PM Checklist

- Assign a revision class before drafting.
- Tell the Writer what must be preserved.
- Name the exact friction point, not a vague quality target.
- Separate `fix now` from `nice to have`.
- Do not ask for more than one structural move in a single pass.
- If round 1 solves the problem, stop.
- If round 2 still struggles, leave it for human review.

## Writer Checklist

- Start from the assigned base text.
- Preserve the hook if it already works.
- Preserve the closer if it already works.
- Fix the middle first.
- Prefer deletion over elaboration.
- Keep the conceptual spine unchanged unless PM explicitly allows otherwise.
- Do not solve density by splitting one idea into two thinner slides.
- Return the smallest revision that actually fixes the issue.

## Editor Checklist

- Judge the revised text, not the draft history.
- Check both quality and edit distance.
- Flag over-revision as a failure, not an achievement.
- Ask whether each body slide is necessary.
- Ask whether the post now reads cleanly on a phone.
- Ask whether the closer is a reframe or just a recap.
- Prefer a pass when the remaining issue is minor and likely better handled by a human copy editor.

## Patterns From The WP6 Pilot

The WP6 pilot produced three repeatable lessons:

1. Preserve the hook; fix the middle.
   Many WP6 hooks were already among the bank’s strongest lines. Most gains came from cutting density or moving the real payoff forward.

2. Reduce catalog mode with one cut, not a full rewrite.
   `aetius-people` improved when the named-example chain was reduced to a shaped escalation rather than expanded into more evidence.

3. Keep the post attached to its real engine.
   `susinum` only works as a recipe post when Paul’s own medical classification remains visible. Without that, it collapses into an etymology post already covered elsewhere.

4. Process posts need process early.
   `mendesian-paul` strengthened when the non-boiled technique moved ahead of the naming lore.

5. Quantity can stay if quantity is the argument.
   `church-incense` and `moschos` remain list- and unit-heavy, but the list is the point, so the revision work is framing and trimming, not de-listing.

## Series-Level Application For Remaining WPs

### Lexical / philological posts

- Move from word to world quickly.
- Do not let uncertainty loops replace payoff.
- Keep the bodily, ritual, or social consequence visible.

### Recipe posts

- Decide whether the spine is process, scale, composition, or transmission.
- Lead with that spine.
- Only keep ingredient inventory if it earns the point.

### Marketplace posts

- Prefer one transaction or comparison plus one escalation.
- Avoid turning the middle into named-source traffic.
- Social meaning should come from the evidence, not from added commentary.

### Philosophy / theory posts

- Anchor theory in anatomy, craft, or procedure.
- One abstraction per slide.
- End on the consequence, not the terminology.

### Myth / literary posts

- Keep narrative movement primary.
- Do not diagram the myth when telling it would do.

## Acceptance Checklist For Remaining WPs

A draft is ready for handoff when:

- the hook is clear and not inflated
- every body slide is necessary
- no slide asks the reader to process too many unfamiliar items at once
- the post has at least one concrete anchor
- the closer does more than summarize
- the voice sounds like the project, not like a copywriter or a lecturer
- the revision did not disturb a strong existing spine

If those conditions are mostly met, stop editing.
